# Owner quality reference, 2026-11-16

The owner sent two public clips of League of Legends match footage (a 58 s player clip and a full pro-view match) as "strong examples of what this game should look like, graphic wise, ui, and effects".

They are a GAUGE for the quality bar, never a source. Nothing from them may be copied: no characters, abilities, icons, art, layout pixel-for-pixel, sounds or names. No frames from them are stored in this repo. What follows is the lead's reading of WHY that footage looks finished, turned into targets VALE must hit in its own style (dial, lantern, chalk and glass motifs; original fighters and kits).

## What makes the reference read as professional

### World and rendering
1. The ground is mid-value and calm. Grass and stone sit in the middle of the value range with low chroma and soft painted variation, so everything that matters sits on top of it. VALE's floor is too dark and too busy in places.
2. Paths, river and jungle are told apart by value and hue at a glance, with soft painted edges, not hard texture seams.
3. Lighting is flat and readable from above: soft ambient, a gentle key, small contact shadows under every unit. No heavy black shadow pools, no crushed darks.
4. Props and walls are chunky, with clear silhouettes and a lighter top face than side face, so walkable versus blocked is obvious.
5. Fog of war is a soft, even dim with a smooth edge, never noisy.

### Fighters and units
6. Fighters are the most saturated and highest-contrast things on screen. Each one is recognisable from its silhouette and two or three colour masses at gameplay zoom.
7. Every unit has a thin dark edge or rim separation from the ground, plus a team-coloured health bar that is large, segmented and always legible.
8. Minions are plainly smaller, simpler and less saturated than fighters; team colour is unmistakable.
9. Animation has strong anticipation and a clear hit frame; attacks read from the pose alone.

### Effects
10. Effects are bright, saturated and SHAPED: each ability has a clear geometric read (line, cone, circle, orb) with a hard readable edge and a soft glowing core.
11. Effects are short. They flash at the important frame, then clear within a fraction of a second so the fight stays readable. Little lingering smoke.
12. Colour belongs to the caster: one or two signature hues per fighter, with white-hot cores at impact.
13. Ground indicators (range circles, skill-shot lines) are thin, bright, semi-transparent and sit flat on the ground under the units.
14. Hits give feedback on the target: a flash, a small burst and floating damage numbers that are colour-coded, sized by amount and never cover the health bar.
15. Kills and objectives get a banner at the top centre with portraits, shown briefly.

### HUD and UI
16. The HUD is compact and anchored bottom-centre: portrait, health and resource bars with numbers, ability icons with clear cooldown sweeps and key labels, items beside them. It occupies little of the screen and has dark semi-opaque plates with thin metallic keylines.
17. The minimap is in a bottom corner, high contrast, with clear team-coloured icons.
18. Top bar: score, timer and team portraits, small and unobtrusive.
19. Typography is one clean family, consistent sizes, strong contrast; numbers are tabular.
20. The cursor and click feedback are obvious: a clear move marker where you click.

## How verifiers must use this

Each wave-2 verifier scores VALE's own captures (the orch1 visual pack) against these twenty targets, one line each: met, partly, or not met, with the frame that shows it. A lane passes its look bar only when no target it owns is "not met". The verifier must also confirm originality: VALE's solution to each target is its own design.
