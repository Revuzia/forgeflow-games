# CUT2: TRUE CUT, real geometric pieces

**Status:** owner spec, written 2026-10-08. It SUPERSEDES the parts of [`CUT.md`](CUT.md) listed in section 0 and keeps everything else in CUT.md. It is built by the PHYS, RENDER and SHELL lanes (AUDIO only verifies), against optional, additive contract members marked `CUT2` in `src/contracts.ts` (section 10). It was written from three read-only design memos in `_handoff/design/` (`CUT2_PHYS.md`, `CUT2_RENDER.md`, `CUT2_SHELL.md`), and every claim it relies on about the current code was re-read by the spec author (appendix A, with `path:line`). Where the memos disagreed, section 17 records the decision and the reason. Everything here is our own work: our gestures, our look, our sounds.

**The owner's request (2026-10-08):** "when I SPLIT, it just takes out a little ball and the squishy itself splits, but I wanted a REAL cut, like if I cut a squishy in half, one eye is on one side and one is on the other. These are clean cuts, not a little ball/piece."

**What was wrong, in one paragraph (verified in the code, appendix A).** Today a cut pinches the body and then swaps in two NEW bodies: a copy of the whole species shrunk by the cube root of its share (the "face piece", `softbody.ts:279-292`) and an eyeless 1.1 x 0.85 x 1.1 ellipsoid "chunk" (`shape.ts:239-275`) whose flat face is only a transient goal that rounds over in 2 s (`softbody.ts:704-710`, `:2373`). So a Split in two gives a small whole squishy with both eyes plus a ball. The chunk also launched itself at 1.5 to 14 m/s because its flat-face goal was never re-centred (`shape.ts:282-294`; cause reproduced by three engineers and again by the spec author, appendix A.3).

**What this spec builds.** Every piece is the real geometric part of the original squishy that lies on its side of the cut plane: the same silhouette on that side, a clean flat cut face where the plane went through, and everything that sat on that side stays with it. A centre cut between the eyes gives one eye on each half. No ball, no shrunken copy, no "face piece versus chunk" roles.

**Revision 2 (2026-10-08): the red-team fold.** Two read-only red teams attacked the first version (`_handoff/design/CUT2_RED_FEASIBILITY.md`: 4 MAJOR, 13 MINOR; `CUT2_RED_PRODUCT.md`: 3 MAJOR, 12 MINOR). The spec author re-verified every finding against the code or by re-running the numbers; section 18 is the finding-by-finding ledger (accepted, accepted in part with what was rejected and why, and where the text changed) and appendix A.6 lists what was re-run. The changes that matter to a reader of the first version: (1) **every piece is exactly ONE convex cell** (a node of the cut tree), and a join of two pieces always brings back the piece they were both cut from; the union builder is NOT built, because its output measured IoU 0.84 median and 39 % reverted rims (3.3, 4.6, 7.2, 17.2 question 1). (2) The foot can no longer push a piece across its own cut plane, and a build that fails the mesh gate is retried and then refused (4.1 steps 8 and 12). (3) The perf promise is held by a particle rule at six pieces (3.2). (4) The first Split in two on a whole squishy cuts along the squishy's OWN line between the eyes whatever the camera shows (6.6). (5) The join commit has a render design (8.8). (6) Joining is a Hand-tool gesture, and the text now says so (7.1, 9.3). (7) Several bars were re-specified with the measurement that moved them (X04, X13, X23). Where a sentence below carries "(RT: PF3)" or "(RT: PR-02)" it is changed text and the tag names the finding.

## 2. Rules that keep the toy honest

1. **True parts.** A piece is the part of the original squishy (its rest silhouette, sampled finer than the game's own detail-3 mesh) on its side of the cut planes. Its rest shape has a flat cut face where each plane went through; that face is part of the REST shape, not a transient goal. Every position-bound thing (eyes, core, glitter, constellation stars, pattern coordinates) belongs to the piece that holds that place.
2. **Cutting never creates items.** Pieces are a play state of the one squishy in your hands. They never appear in the Hoard, never count as copies, and cannot be merged, traded or kept. (CUT.md rule 1.)
3. **Volume is exact.** The fractions of all pieces sum to 1 within 1e-9, each piece's built rest volume is `frac x V3` within 1e-9 (V3 is the detail-3 whole's rest volume, the game's definition of "the whole"), and the planned fraction equals the built one.
4. **Limits.** At most 6 pieces on `high` and `med`, 4 on `low`. No piece under 1/8 of the whole (tolerance 1e-4, so the exact halves of a 1/4 piece pass). A refused cut gives the small wobble and one line, in this order: `small` ("Too small to cut there. Try nearer the middle." / "That piece is as small as it gets."), `limit` ("That's as many pieces as it can make."; also when the cut would make a SIXTH piece while one of the six would be a detail-3 body: the particle rule of 3.2), `thin` (the thinner part's aspect under 0.08, or the builder cannot make a part cleanly after its retries, 4.1 step 12: "That would make a piece too thin. Try another angle."), `loose` (the cut would split off two separate parts, so a part is not one body: "That cut would split off two separate parts. Try another angle."). Two different lines for a miss, as in the shipped game (`hudBinding.ts:245`, `:331`): a swipe that misses says "Swipe across the squishy to cut it." (a swipe under 24 px, a tap, says nothing: `cut.ts:496`); Split in two with nothing to split says "Nothing to cut there." (RT: PF1, PF2, PR-15).
5. **Leaving reconnects.** Switching squishy, opening the Hoard, a capsule or a merge, hiding the tab over 60 s, or a reload puts the squishy back together first (the 1.2 s animation if it is on screen, instantly otherwise). Nothing about pieces is stored. (CUT.md rule 2.)
6. **No economy change.** Touches on pieces pay the meter exactly as touches on a whole squishy (pokes, squeezes, pulls, the same freshness rule). Cutting, joining and the morphs make no SoftEvent and pay nothing. (CUT.md rule 3.)
7. **Cut faces stay flat.** No round-over transient, for every family. The rim of a cut is a true edge (an edge loop on the plane and on the silhouette); only the renderer's normal smoothing softens it.
8. **Calm effects** (DESIGN 6.6): a softer seam glow, shorter strands, no shake, the parting gap x 0.6, and no camera move (the optional peek of owner question 3 is off in Calm). There is never a screen flash. The flash governor applies to every cut light. (CUT.md rule 5.)
9. **Accessibility.** With the squishy focused, the Cut tool's popover offers **Split in two** and **Reconnect all**: buttons, reachable by keyboard, announced in the live region ("Cut into N pieces.", "Whole again."). The tool never needs a drag. Five presses of Split in two reach six pieces on every species (the tolerance fix of 6.6). **Undo cut** (owner question 5, built by default) joins the two pieces of the most recent cut, which is always an exact twin join, so a keyboard player can step back one cut at a time without a drag; it announces "Joined. Now N pieces." A keyboard player pokes only the anchor piece (the play-target ring sits on it) and cannot join two chosen pieces: the accepted limit of CUT.md, stated here. (CUT.md rule 6; RT: PR-14.)
10. **Any two pieces can join, and the result is always a real part: the piece they were both cut from.** Pieces that were cut apart from each other (twins) fit exactly; the cut between them disappears. Two pieces that were not twins join through their common parent, and every piece cut from that parent comes along. Every piece is one convex cell of the squishy, so every result is a cell that was a piece (or the whole) earlier and a valid body. Joining the last pieces gives the root: a fresh `createBody(genome)` (section 7). No union of non-twin cells is ever built (3.3, RT: PF3).
11. **A piece rests, it does not launch.** In the swap-flow sweep (X13) no piece rises more than 0.06 m or moves up faster than 1.0 m/s; supported pieces stay under 1.2 m/s from 0.1 s and under 0.3 m/s from 1 s, except the named categories of X13 (a piece falling from its built height, a thin slice propped on its rim, a sloped face sliding, an upper cap sliding off a level stack, stacked tilted pieces pressing into each other).
12. **Whole bodies are untouched.** Until the first `setNeck` a whole body is bit-for-bit what it is today (X21).
13. **Deterministic and allocation-free.** The builder is a pure function of (genome, region, detail). The scripted parting uses sim time only. No allocation per step in the solver, the morphs, the parting or the intent machine (building and joining are events and may allocate).
14. **One swipe cuts one piece** (the one the swipe passes over most). Cutting every piece a swipe crosses is not built (the 6-piece and 1/8 bookkeeping stay obvious and every refusal stays explainable).

## 3. The model

### 3.1 Frames and units

* **W, the whole's rest frame.** Origin = the rest mass centroid (tributary-area centroid) of the detail-3 whole, axes = the whole's rest axes (y up), metres. This is the frame of `new SoftBody(genome).restLocal` (`shape.ts:193-231` centres on the mass-weighted centroid) and is the only frame planes are ever stored in.
* **Piece rest-local frame.** `restLocal_piece[i] = x_W[i] - offset`, exactly (a translation: no rotation and no scale is ever baked in), where `offset` is the mass centroid of the piece's own rest mesh in W. Every piece therefore has the same axes as the whole, and nested cuts, merges and plane pull-back are pure translations.
* **World frame.** `x_world = center + R(frame) x restLocal_piece`, with `center`, `frame` as `SoftBodyLike` publishes them.

### 3.2 Source solid, cells, regions, fractions

* **Source solid S.** The whole's rest mesh at icosphere detail 4 (2562 vertices, 5120 triangles), built by the same `restPoint` recipe path (`buildRest(genome, buildIcosphere(4))`) and shifted into W. It is INTERNAL to the physics, cached per (species, size), and is the only geometry any piece is cut from, however many cuts deep, so nothing compounds. (Why detail 4: a detail-3 source gives pieces 2.9 % short of the finest clip at the median, detail 4 gives 1.9 %; CUT2_PHYS 2.1.)
* **`PieceInfo.whole` is NOT S.** It is the detail-3 whole mesh the game already uses (see 10 and 17 #1).
* **Plane.** `RestPlane { id, n, d }`, unit `n`, in W; side A is `n . p >= d`. Ids start at 1 and are never reused until the squishy is whole again.
* **Cell.** The intersection of half-spaces named by signed ids (+id: side A of cut `id`, -id: side B), with S. **Region** = a union of cells in the TYPES (so a later union builder needs no contract change, tuning item T9); **in this build every region the shell asks for is exactly ONE cell** (3.3). `[[]]` (one cell, no literal) = the whole. (RT: PF3.)
* **Fractions** are shares of V3. A piece's `frac` is its built `restVolume / V3`.
* **Detail.** A piece with `frac < 0.35` is built at icosphere detail 2 (162 particles), else detail 3 (642), as CUT.md 4.3 said. The measured cost of the alternatives (six pieces at detail 3 from 0.2: 1.14 to 1.44 times two whole bodies; detail 3 for all: 4.2 to 4.4) is why this stays.
* **The particle rule at six pieces (RT: PF1; this is how "6 pieces cost no more than 2 whole bodies" is kept).** Two whole bodies are 1284 particles. With six pieces of at least 1/8 a detail-3 body can exist only if one piece is in [0.35, 0.3755] and the other five are each in [0.1249, 0.1504] (0.35 + 5 x 0.1249 <= 1): the state costs 642 + 5 x 162 = 1452 particles and measured **1.52 and 1.64 times two whole bodies** on kneadle (best of 10, shipped solver, `collide` every frame; the red team measured 1.11 to 1.37), while every other six-piece state is all detail 2 (972 particles, measured 0.80 times). That corner is about 1e-5 of the space of six-piece partitions and cannot be reached by Split in two (it always halves the largest piece), only by five deliberate swipes that each leave a piece within 0.025 of the minimum. **Rule: a cut that would make the sixth piece while any of the six is, or would be built as, detail 3 is refused with the `limit` line.** The detail of a live piece is its `vertexCount` (642 or 162); a child's is `frac >= 0.35`. Not rejected, only documented: five pieces hold at most one detail-3 piece (642 + 4 x 162 = 1290, 1.005 times two bodies, inside the measuring noise); three and four pieces may hold two (up to 1446 and 1608 particles, 1.13 and 1.25 times); the owner's bar names six, X08 reports the others and the shipped CUT.md had the same profile. Demoting a live detail-3 piece to detail 2 at the fifth cut was considered and rejected: it needs a second body-rebuild path with state transfer and a view swap on a piece the player did not touch, for a corner nobody reaches.

### 3.3 The cut tree (the shell's truth)

The shell keeps an explicit binary tree: `Node { id, parent, kids: [A, B] | null, lits: Literal[] }`, the root is the whole, a leaf has no kids. **Every live piece is exactly one LEAF and its region is exactly ONE convex cell** (S cut by the half-spaces on the path from the root). A cut splits the one leaf it is applied to into two kids. A join of pieces P and Q takes `N = lca(P, Q)`, joins every piece under N (all the leaves under N are live pieces) and turns N back into a leaf: the merged piece is N's cell, literal for literal, whatever happened in between. Consequences, each a property test (X18): joining all pieces in any order ends at `{ root }`; a twin join returns exactly the parent's cell; the group of any two pieces is a node of the tree; the tree has at most 6 live leaves and 11 nodes. Planes are floats in W that are never compared; a node's plane is dropped when the node becomes a leaf again and the table is reset when the squishy is whole. (RT: PF3, PF13: the first version let a piece be a SET of leaves, which needed the union builder, `cellFa`, the leaf-count bound of 64 that the red team showed is not an invariant of that algebra, and a classifier that could leave a 1 % sliver on the wrong side of a cut. None of that exists any more.)

**Why no multi-cell pieces.** Two touching pieces that are not twins (for example one half and a quarter of the other half) could in principle join as just those two, a union of two cells. The builder of 4.1 is written for one convex cell, and the red team measured what it does with a union: of 72 non-twin unions built on random cut trees the median IoU against the true region was 0.84 (minimum 0.42), the rim snap reverted on 39 % (28 of 72), 37 had more than ten non-star triangles and the skin was pushed up to 0.97 R0 to keep the volume exact (CUT2_RED_FEASIBILITY PF3). The spec author re-ran the controlled cases (half + quarter, half + eighth) on 50 species: the rim snap reverted on 41 of 50 in both (A.6). Twin joins (IoU 0.97, rim reverted 1 of 27) and common-parent groups (IoU 0.975, 0 of 11 reverted) are safe. Choosing the safe set costs a behaviour: joining two non-twins brings back their common parent (7.2, owner question 1). A union builder with boundary-plane extraction and a per-vertex cap test, gated on the BUILT target (IoU >= 0.93, rim clean, no non-star triangle) and falling back to the group rule, is tuning item T9; it needs no contract change.

### 3.4 The cut plane in three frames

The swipe gives a world plane. `planCut` pulls it back into the body's rest frame ONCE and the physics LATCHES it: `n_r = R^T n`, `d_r = n . (p - c)` (R = best-fit frame, c = centre of mass), then `n_W = n_r`, `d_W = d_r + n_r . offset`. The latched plane drives the neck, the prediction and the children, so the pinch, the planned fractions and the pieces are one plane (today `neckGoal` re-derives the rest plane from the current frame every frame, `softbody.ts:2453-2456`, and `measureCut` clips the CURRENT deformed mesh, `:1259-1293`). The world plane the seam glow follows is recomputed every step from the latched plane: `n_world = R n_W`, `point_world = center + R (n_W d_W - offset)`. The one thing that may still move the latched plane is the builder's retry ladder (4.1 step 12): it may shift `d_W` by at most 0.012 R0 (3 mm at R0 0.5; invisible) when a part fails the mesh gate, and it updates `plan.fa`, `offA/B` and `velA/B` in the same step, so prediction = construction still holds for the plane that is built.

### 3.5 The anchor piece

`BodyManager.body` must be something (the stage's primary view, the gravity setting target, the keyboard's default poke target). It is the **anchor**: the larger child of the last cut of the anchor (tie: side A). It has no visible role: identity (genome, item, nickname, tier), HUD, history, Hoard and camera framing do not depend on it. Cutting any other piece never changes the anchor. A cut of the anchor re-points the play body with the new `BodyManager.promote(body)` and the stage's new `setPrimaryBody(id)`; a cut NEVER calls `stage.setBody` (it removes and re-adds every view, `stage.ts:651-666`, and `bodies.ts:116` re-adds every extra on a commit, 25 to 80 ms per view build by RENDER's measurement).

## 4. Physics geometry: how a piece's rest mesh is built (PHYS; new pure modules `src/physics/piece.ts`, `src/physics/region.ts`)

Chosen design: **radial re-mesh of the clipped part from its own centroid on the unchanged icosphere topology** (the hard constraint of the brief: no solver change, 642 particles, 162 under 0.35). The rejected alternative, keeping the whole's own vertices and projecting the removed ones onto the plane, was measured by PHYS: it folds the cap over itself (83 of 102 regions had inverted triangles, minimum angle 0) and cannot be repaired by any bevel. A Laplacian bevel to make the rim "soft" was measured to erode thin pieces (volume -36 %, the flat face shrinks to 38 % of the true section) and is NOT used: the rim of a true cut is a 90 to 140 degree edge and the solver is made to accept it (5.3).

`buildPiece(genome, region, detail, frac)` is a pure function (no three, no DOM, no `Math.random`, no `Date`, fixed iteration counts, cell lists sorted by literal). It returns a `RestShape` exactly as `shape.ts:24-45` defines it (`restLocal, floppy, feature, strutW, mass, restRadius, restCenterY, peakVertex, restVolume, height`) plus `{ offset, rayOrigin, cells }`. The constructor then builds everything else (Q, masses, foot list, edge and hinge rest lengths, struts, memory arm) from it unchanged.

### 4.1 The algorithm (constants are the prototype's, to be re-confirmed on the port by the probe rows of section 13)

1. **Ray stack.** For a ray `o + t u`: sorted crossings of S's triangles (uniform 14^3 grid over S's box, Moller-Trumbore, same-sign duplicates on a shared edge merged) give the inside intervals `[a_k, b_k]`. Each cell clips them to the span of its half-spaces: for plane `(n, d)`, `s0 = n . o - d`, `den = n . u`; `den > 0`: `t >= -s0/den`; `den < 0`: `t <= s0/(-den)`; `|den| < 1e-14`: empty if `s0 < 0`. A union merges the cells' interval lists (touching within 1e-9 are one). `exit(o, u)` = the end of the interval that starts at 0.
2. **Centre `c`.** For a child of `planCut`: the exact volume centroid of the parent's REST mesh clipped by the plane (tetrahedra from a point on the plane, extended with the tetra centroids; no rays). For a region built from its cells alone (a join, a reload): `V = 1/3 int sum(b^3 - a^3) dOmega`, `c = (1/4 int sum(b^4 - a^4) u dOmega) / V` over 600 Fibonacci directions (valid for any origin and any shape).
3. **Soundness (star-shapedness).** `phi(c) = (1/V)(1/3) int exit(c, u)^3 dOmega` over 360 directions: the share of the region's volume that straight rays from `c` reach. If `phi < 0.97`: deterministic centre search (160-point Fibonacci scan of S's box, then a 5-round pattern search, at most 190 evaluations). The region is SOUND iff the best `phi >= 0.95`. An unsound region is NOT one body and is refused (4.4). The missing at most 5 % (a shadowed ear tip) is simply not meshed; step 9 puts its volume back into the rest of the skin.
4. **Seed.** `x_i = c + exit(c, u_i) u_i` for the icosphere's direction vectors `IcoMesh.dirs`: topology exactly today's, star-shaped about `c` by construction.
5. **Radial relaxation, K = 4 passes, lambda = 0.7.** `m_i` = mean of the 1-ring; `u' = normalise(x_i + lambda (m_i - x_i) - c)`; `x_i <- c + exit(c, u') u'`. Every vertex stays exactly on the skin or exactly on its cap plane; the density equalises between the dense middle of a flat face (seen from an inside point a flat face is a gnomonic stretch, spacing grows with sec^2) and its rim.
6. **Rim snap** (the clean edge the owner wants). A vertex is "on plane p" when `|n . x - d| < 1e-8 R0`. The cap's triangles are those with all three vertices on p; its boundary vertices `B_p` are the on-p vertices touching a non-cap triangle; `g_p` = their mean. Each `i in B_p` slides OUTWARD in the plane, `w = normalise(proj_p(x_i - g_p))`, to the silhouette: `x_i <- x_i + w min(t, 0.9 e_i)`, `t = exit(x_i + 1e-7 R0 n, w)`, `e_i` the vertex's mean edge. After it the rim is an edge loop lying on the plane AND on S's skin (within 1e-7 R0) and no triangle mixes cap and skin vertices across the rim. **Corner rules for multi-cut pieces** (found by PHYS's cut-tree sweep: without them 441 of 560 pieces reverted the snap): a vertex already on two or more cap planes is fixed in the snap and the cap relaxation, and a rim vertex stops 0.3 of its mean edge short of every OTHER cap plane it moves toward.
7. **Cap interior and skin.** 12 in-plane Laplacian passes (lambda 0.8) on on-plane vertices that are not boundary; 4 radial passes (lambda 0.5) on vertices on no plane (a skin vertex whose new direction would end on a cap plane stays, so classes never change). **Validity gate:** no tetrahedron `(c, tri)` inverted and every triangle area > 1e-4 of the mean; otherwise the snap is undone to the step-5 mesh and the piece is reported `rimClean = false`.
8. **Foot (adaptive).** If fewer than 7 vertices lie within 0.004 R0 of the lowest point, flatten the bottom: floor `f = max(y_min + 0.025 (y_max - y_min), the 8th-lowest vertex height)` (so at least 7 vertices land exactly on it); `t = y - f`; `y' = f` (t <= 0), `f + t^2/(2k)` (0 < t < k), `y - k/2` (t >= k); `k = 0.035 R0` (the width of `shape.ts:70-76`). A piece with a cap down or the old foot already has a flat patch and is left alone. **The foot never moves a vertex that lies on a cap plane** (a vertical shift takes it off an oblique plane and across it: 22 of 114 pieces overlapped their sibling before this rule). **Two more rules (RT: PF2; both measured by the spec author, A.6):** (a) **room clamp** - the vertical move of every other vertex is limited exactly as step 9 limits the volume offset: for each cap plane `p` (kept side `sd = n . x - d >= 0`) a move whose component toward the plane is `c = dy n_y < 0` may not exceed `room = max(0, sd - min(sd, 0.005 R0))`, so a skin vertex next to an OBLIQUE cap can no longer be carried across it; (b) **lift cap** - if reaching the floor needs `f - y_min > FOOT_LIFT_MAX` (0.08 R0; the whole's own foot patch is 0.035 R0 wide) the foot is NOT added: the lowest part of such a piece is a point or an edge (a horn, a sliver's tip), and flattening it cut up to 0.6 R0 off the piece. A thin slice cut off the side therefore usually has nothing flat to stand on and rests propped on its rim (1 to 27 degrees, 3 to 5 particles on the table; 5.2) - decision 17 #8, reworded. On the 19 planes where the red team's sweep found a piece across its own plane (8 of 1810 sides, worst 0.35 R0) or overlapping its sibling (11 sides, worst 0.22 R0), the room clamp alone gives 0 and 0, with or without the lift cap (A.6). On 40 ordinary planes (80 sides, nothing flagged) the room clamp changes nothing, and the lift cap of 0.08 R0 removes the foot from 11 of the 47 sides that had one (a cap of 0.05 from 27 of 47): FOOT_LIFT_MAX is a STARTING value; L2 (3 poses x 12 families, >= 7 particles on the table) and X13 re-run with it, and P8 raises it (to at most 0.12 R0) if a pose falls under 7 particles.
9. **Exact volume without a scale.** `restLocal + offset` must remain the W-position of the same material, so a uniform scale is out. The skin vertices (those on no cap plane) move together by `delta` along their area-weighted vertex normals; `V(delta)` is monotone and is solved by 14 secant steps to 1e-12. The component of a skin vertex's move TOWARD a cap plane is limited so it keeps `min(its distance, 0.005 R0)` from it (inside the volume loop, so the volume is still exact). Prototype `|delta|`: median 0.0002 R0, p95 0.0062, max 0.0118 at detail 3. **A piece never leaves its half-spaces:** 0 of 114 pieces overlapped their sibling with these rules (22 of 114 without them); that sample did not contain 1/8 slivers, planes through thin features or planes through the foot, and on the red team's 1810 builds of exactly those families 8 sides still left their half-space until the room clamp of step 8 was added (RT: PF2). Do not project crossing vertices onto the plane (aspect ratios of 1e16, 29 wrong-facing triangles in the prototype).
10. **Normalise.** Tributary-area masses `m_i` (a third of each incident triangle, mean 1), mass centroid `c_m`, `offset = c_m`, `restLocal_i = x_i - c_m`, one more numeric re-centre so `sum m_i restLocal_i = 0` to 1e-17, `restRadius = R0 cbrt(frac)`, `restCenterY = -min y`, `height`, `peakVertex`.
11. **Weights.** From the recipe at each vertex's W-direction from the recipe origin: `floppyOf`, `floppyFeature`, `floppySecond` (`shape.ts:117-137`) and `strutWeight` (`:151-153`); a cap vertex deep inside the body is not a thin tip, so the weight is faded by `sstep(0.55, 0.9, rho / r_skin(dir))` (`rho` = distance from the recipe origin, `r_skin` = `evalShape(dir) R0`). `buildStruts` runs unchanged on the new mesh: a cut ear keeps its struts and its flop.
12. **Post-conditions, the retry ladder and the refusal (RT: PF2).** Every build is checked before it is cached: (i) every vertex is on the kept side of every cap plane to 1e-12 R0; (ii) every tributary mass lies in [0.01, 8] of the mean (this is the G4 bar of 13.1, now a gate: the red team found 14 of 1810 builds below it, down to 8.7e-6, all with a reverted rim and all at detail 2, and the solver turned them into folds of 165 to 176 degrees and a volume loss of 6 %; the spec author reproduced those runs, A.6); (iii) no inverted tetrahedron `(c, tri)` and no triangle under 1e-4 of the mean area; (iv) the exact volume (1e-12); (v) the skin offset `|delta|` <= 0.05 R0 (nested pieces reach 0.15, 4.3). A reverted rim snap (`rimClean = false`) is NOT a failure by itself when (i) to (v) hold. **On a failure the ladder rebuilds with the latched plane's `d` shifted by +0.004, -0.004, +0.008, -0.008, +0.012, -0.012 R0** (skipping any shift that would put a part under 1/8 - 1e-4 or move a share by more than 0.01); the first build that passes is cached and the plan is updated (3.4). If none passes, the plane is REFUSED: `prepareCut` reports the failure, the neck relaxes and the shell says the `thin` line (2 rule 4). Measured on the 19 planes where the red team's sweep found a flagged side, with the foot rules of step 8: 14 planes still failed the gate, a shift of at most 0.012 R0 repaired 7 of them, and rebuilding the failing sides at detail 3 repaired 1 of 19 sides (so the cause is the plane against that species' shape, not the mesh resolution; the 14 failing planes are capnap 3, wisplet 2, and one each for nuzzo, wrigglo, hushpuff, taffelin, selenuff, caromel, tidelume, glimglop, somnuff: 11 species, none of the other 39). The 7 that stay refused are 0.8 % of the 905 planes of that sweep (uniformly random planes 1 of 250, 1/8 slivers 3 of 250, planes through the foot 3 of 250, planes through thin features 0 of 155). The 0.8 % was measured with gates (i) to (iv) and the mass gate; gate (v) adds little to single planes (4 of 1810 sides have `|delta|` over 0.05 R0: glimglop 0.077, capnap 0.054, selenuff 0.053, hushpuff 0.051; three of them already fail the mass gate) but flags **9 of the 560 nested pieces (1.6 %)**, which the ladder may or may not repair; P1b measures the combined refusal rate for single and nested planes and reports it. Probe rows C2, G3 and G7 report the refusal rate per species and per family; the refusal is a measured rate, not a bar, and a rate above 3 % of nested cuts is reported to the orchestrator with the numbers (the 0.05 is a tuning value) and is never loosened silently by an engineer.

### 4.3 Nested cuts

A cut of a piece adds a half-space in W (`d_W = d_r + n_r . offset`). The child's region is S cut by the whole list of half-spaces on its path in the tree, built from S, never from the parent's re-meshed polyhedron. The child's FRACTION comes from the exact clip of the parent's rest mesh (`planCut`), its GEOMETRY from S; they differ by the parent's re-mesh error and step 9 removes it by moving the skin. The first version said "at most 1.5 %, under 0.005 R0 of skin offset"; the numbers are wider (RT: PF8, both checked by the spec author, A.6): the mesh share and the cell share of the same split differ by p50 0.14 %, max 0.80 % of the parent, which is p50 0.30 %, p95 1.0 %, max 3.0 % of the child (13 cuts, 3 trees), and the 560 nested pieces of PHYS's own sweep (all detail 2) have `|delta|` median 0.0108, p95 0.0314, **max 0.152 R0** (knubby, four cap planes), 46 % over the single-plane maximum of 0.0118. A 0.15 R0 bulge on a piece of radius 0.5 R0 is visible and is what drags the nested IoU to 0.86, so `|delta|` <= 0.05 R0 is a build post-condition (4.1 step 12) with the retry ladder behind it. Prototype: 100 random cut trees, 460 accepted cuts, 560 pieces with 1 to 5 cap planes: IoU vs the clipped source median 0.957, min 0.864; no flipped tetrahedron; nested pieces are visibly coarser than first-generation ones (most are small and at detail 2), which the filmstrip judges (task R4).

### 4.4 Thin features, and the one thing that is refused

A plane may cut anywhere, including through an eye, an ear, a horn, a tail or a petal; the region is geometry, so nothing special-cases a feature and a stub of it stays on each side (its floppy and strut weights come from the recipe inside the region). Star-shapedness about the centroid held for every single-plane region except three of 2213: a level plane at +0.5 R0 on **munchip** and **selenuff** takes BOTH big mirrored ears off at once (two islands; the centroid lies between them, outside the material: 3.6 % and 4.3 % of the part reachable) and an oblique 0.5 R0 plane on **ambrosel** (93 %). Those are `sound = false` and the shell refuses them with the `loose` line ("That cut would split off two separate parts. Try another angle."; the first wording, "loose crumbs", described two big ears as crumbs: RT: PR-15). The refusal is concentrated: the 0.14 % above is an average over a grid of planes; the red team's scan of 8 swipe angles x 76 offsets x 50 species (19 522 eligible swipes, re-analysed by the spec author, A.6) found 21 refusals, **all on selenuff, and all on near-horizontal swipes at +0.375 to +0.55 R0: 7 of the 48 to 50 eligible offsets (14 %) at each of the three angles around horizontal**, i.e. the natural "cut the head off" swipe on that one species; "cut both ears off" is refused on three species (munchip, selenuff, ambrosel). Nothing else in the 15 000 camera and uniform planes was unsound. This is accepted as a known limit (decision 20) and listed in the owner's notes; a union builder (T9) would not change it, a "two-body piece" would (not built). The test is applied to both children of every cut (a node's cell is one cell), which is what makes every join target in section 7 a region that was a piece before and is therefore buildable. No bridge, no web, no hull: a fake shape would break "everything on that side stays with it". (A thin feature alone is usually far under 1/8 of the volume, so an ear can normally not be cut off BY ITSELF; the line then says "Too small to cut there.")

### 4.5 Caches, cost, and the resumable build

* **Cache** of built rest shapes keyed by a string of (species, size, region literals with the planes' 17-digit floats, detail): identical inputs return the SAME arrays, so a re-merge of twins returns the parent's rest shape EXACTLY when the detail is the same (a parent of 0.35 or more was built at detail 3 and its two children at detail 2, so a twin join of two detail-2 children is a new detail-2 build of the parent's cell: same cell, literal for literal, not the same mesh; RT: PF15). LRU of 64 entries, cleared when the squishy is whole again. The source solid S and its grid are cached per (species, size), LRU of 8.
* **Prototype cost** (plain JS, allocation-heavy, loaded machine): detail 3 median 15 ms, p95 76 ms per piece; detail 2 median 4 ms, p95 20 ms; a half 20 to 73 ms warm; union regions up to 412 ms. **Port budget (hard):** median <= 12 ms and p95 <= 40 ms per detail-3 piece in the browser, one resumable stage <= 3 ms. Levers: typed scratch arrays, the grid cached per species, no per-ray allocation, fused relaxation passes.
* `prepareCut(budgetMs, maxStages?)` and `prepareRegion(region, budgetMs, maxStages?)` run the build as a resumable sequence of stages (soundness search if still pending, seed, 4 relaxations, snap, cap, skin, foot, volume, normalise, weights, gate), once per step, and return true when everything is cached. **Determinism (RT: PF14):** a call does as many stages as fit in `budgetMs`, but never more than `maxStages` when that is given; the node harness and X22 pass `budgetMs = Infinity` and a fixed `maxStages` (for example 2), so the frame at which a build completes, and therefore the frame of the swap, does not depend on wall-clock speed. Production passes only the budget. **`planCut` has a budget too (RT: PF12):** the prototype's soundness work was 5 000 to 75 000 ray queries per nested cut (p50 316 ms, p95 2.6 s on a shared machine), far too much for the swipe frame. `planCut` therefore does the cheap geometric part (clip, `fa`, aspects, velocities, a 360-ray `phi` at the exact clipped centroid of each child; <= 4 ms) and, when that `phi` is under 0.97, returns `sound: true, soundPending: true`; the centre search then runs as the FIRST stage of `prepareCut`. A negative answer arriving during the neck aborts the neck with the `loose` line (a rare path: the band of 4.4 is 0.1 % of swipes). The constructor, `setRegion` and `placeAt` take the cache; a cold first build (JIT) is paid by a prewarm (6.1). If a budget cannot be met the fallbacks are, in order: lengthen the neck hold by at most 0.15 s (6.3), build in the swap frame (a one-off hitch), and last a Web Worker (the builder is pure and works on typed arrays, so it can move; not planned).

## 5. Physics solver changes (PHYS; `softbody.ts`, `shape.ts`)

A piece's rest shape is a `RestShape` like any other, so the loop needs almost nothing new: the shape-matching goal aims at `Q`, so the flat cut face is held exactly as the foot, the belly or an ear is held, because it IS the rest shape. Nothing rounds over in time for a body built with a region: no `flatK`, no `FLAT`, no `ROUND_TAU`. A putty piece keeps its flat face for ever because it IS flat, not because a time constant is long. The legacy build (`{ frac, chunk }` without a region, and the mat's `{ frac: 1, chunk: false, at }`, `boot.ts:111-112`) keeps working until task P9.

### 5.1 The launch fix: every goal has zero mass-weighted mean (the cause, measured four times)

**Cause.** The shape-matching goal is `GOAL_i = c + R QG_i` with `c` the centroid of the predicted positions (`softbody.ts:1950`, `:1979-1982`). A goal whose own mass-weighted mean is not zero pulls the whole body by `K x soft x mean` every substep with no reaction. `flatPoints` pulls the cut-side points onto a plane and rescales about the ORIGIN and never re-centres (`shape.ts:282-294`), so the chunk's `FLAT` goal has a mass-weighted mean of 0.015 to 0.074 R0, by species, fraction and face direction (PHYS E0 over 50 species: 0.015 to 0.056; SHELL M1 and RENDER S6: 0.072 to 0.073 for a side cut; the author: 0.046 to 0.074, appendix A.3). The code base already knows the rule for its other goal terms: the slosh field subtracts its mean explicitly (`softbody.ts:1965-1968`). The neck goal has the same defect (it lowers the waist axis onto the table and swells the lobes away from it, so its mean moves up as t goes to 1, `softbody.ts:2446-2537`, which ends without centring), which is also the known "neck swell" and the whole-body rise during a pinch (SHELL_4_FIX2: boingle's whole body rose from y 0.38 to 1.30 m during the neck).

**Reproduction by the spec author** (appendix A.3): the shipped chunk, 6 species, frac 0.5, cut face to the side: peak centre speed 4.06 to 13.76 m/s as built; with ONLY the `FLAT` goal re-centred, 1.64 to 2.05 m/s (the rig's own 0.3 m drop). Three other independent reproductions agree (PHYS E0: median 3.3 to 0.15 m/s on the table, max 13.6 to 0.43; SHELL M1; RENDER S7). The face-DOWN case keeps 0.8 to 1.6 m/s after centring in PHYS's measurement; the second contributor is the chunk being BUILT at the rounded `Q` while the goal and edge rest lengths already follow `FLAT` (a built-in strain, `softbody.ts:2425-2431`). It is designed out, not patched: a true piece's rest mesh is its final shape, so particles, goal, edge rest lengths and volume agree at t = 0.

**Rule.** `centreGB()`: after `GB` is composed in `frameUpdate` (`softbody.ts:2390-2399`: fold, burst, setFrac scale, flat face, neck) and before the memory arm and `QG` (`:2401-2410`), when the goal has a neck or a morph (`nk > 0 || morphOn`): `GB_i -= (sum_j m_j GB_j) / Mtot`. Fold (max 0.011 R0), burst and setFrac (exactly 0, uniform scales about the origin) are left ALONE so that every whole body stays bit-for-bit what it is until its first `setNeck` (PHYS E11: identical `stateHash` at 33 checkpoints on 12 of 12 species). Cost 6n flops while a neck or morph runs. A debug field `goalDrift` holds the size of the correction; probe L5 asserts `<= 1e-9 R0` after the call and prints the size BEFORE it for every term. (RT: PF6) Centring removes the goal's own pull; it does NOT remove every way a strongly necked body on a tilted plane gains momentum (5.4 states what was measured and what the shell does about it).

**Interim.** `_handoff/reports/SHELL_4_FIX2_physics_goal_centroid.patch` (24 lines, not applied: `grep` for its marker text in `src/` finds nothing) contains this fix for the neck (its second half) and a re-centring of `flatPoints` (its first half, moot once chunks are deleted). Task P0 applies the neck half as `centreGB` now, because CUT2 keeps it and it removes the neck swell; the `flatPoints` half is applied only if a deploy with the old chunks is planned before CUT2 lands.

### 5.2 Resting: the foot, orientation, placement, momentum

* **Placement is by the same definition on both sides.** A piece is born at exactly the W-position of the material it represents: `X_i = c_parent + R(frame) (restLocal_i + offset - offset_parent)`. The solver's centre of mass is the tributary-area surface centroid; the solid's centre of mass differs from it by 0.022 to 0.045 R0 (PHYS, dollop), so using the mesh definition on both sides makes the two cap planes coincide to 1e-9 R0: flush, no gap, no overlap. The shell's old `lobeCentres` (the mean of the particles on each side of the CURRENT deformed mesh) is deleted.
* **Orientation.** `PieceOpts.frame` is the parent's best-fit rotation; the child's particles start rotated by it and `qr` starts equal to it. Without it a tilted parent pops at the swap (a toppled parent of 92 degrees gave children born upright, 0.5 R inside their neighbours).
* **No constructor lift for a body built with a region.** The old constructor lifts a piece whose lowest particle would be below the table BY ITSELF; 3 to 55 mm of difference between siblings overlapped them by 22 to 48 particles. `placeAt` gives exact positions and the shell applies one common lift (6.4 step 3).
* **Momentum is conserved across the cut:** each child gets the mean world velocity of the parent's particles on its side (`velA`, `velB` of the plan), O(n). The physics adds no push (the old `n x 0.22 m/s` push never separated anything: SHELL M2).
* **Rest orientation.** The weeble rights the REST +y axis and nothing else, so a piece rests in its rest orientation: a vertical half stands on the old foot patch, the top half of a level cut sits cap-down (the whole cap is the foot), the lower half sits dome-down. A baked-in topple was considered and rejected (it sweeps corners 0.3 to 0.5 R0 into the table, the same family of launch, and breaks `x_piece = x_W - offset`). Measured by PHYS E2 (12 species, one per family, 3 poses, 4 s): 36 of 36 pieces at rest, peak speed 0.000 m/s, jitter 0.00 mm, 10 or more particles on the table. **A thin slice cut off the side has neither a cap down nor a foot, and it does NOT tip onto its curved side** (the first version said it would; RT: PF7, mechanism read by the spec author at `softbody.ts:2290` and `:2640`): the red team's swap-flow runs on 12 distinct slices of 0.14 to 0.28 (7 species) left every one propped on 3 to 5 rim particles (putty: 8 and 23), tilted 1.3 to 26.9 degrees from upright, its centre 1 to 13 cm below its rest-pose height, moving under 0.04 m/s (putty 0.09 to 0.17 m/s, still creeping at 5 s). Cause: `supp = smooth01((near - 1) / 4)` removes gravity once 5 particles are within 1 cm of the table and gives 16 to 50 % of it at 3 or 4, and the weeble torque (`kUp = 40`) pulls the frame back toward the rest +y, so the slice stays propped and drooped; it is stable, at rest, and X13 passes it (as the named category "propped slice"). If the owner prefers a slice that lies down, the lever is to drop `kUp` for a body with fewer than 5 contact particles (tuning item T10, not built).
* **Mass.** A particle's mass is its tributary area, mean 1 per body, so every body has `Mtot = n`; gravity, shape matching, damping and the self-righting torque are mass-free. Body mass matters only in `collide`, which splits every push half-half; weighting by `frac` was tried and made it worse (4 of 11 cuts passing against 5 of 11), so half-half stays.

### 5.3 The fold limit knows each hinge's rest dihedral

`softbody.ts:1687-1688` computes ONE limit for every hinge: `FINGER.foldMaxDeg` = 110 degrees. A true rim hinge is 100 to 140 degrees AT REST, so a fingertip or the table edge near the rim would hold it "folded" past the limit and the loop would re-open it by force every substep (measured: rim moved 23 to 46 degrees from its rest and the piece kept moving at 33 to 114 mm/s, 2.5 s after the finger left). Fix (data, not logic): three `Float64Array(nh)` (`HC`, `HCH`, `HSH`) filled in the constructor, `lim_h = min(170, max(FINGER.foldMaxDeg, restDih_h + 30))` degrees, and `foldLimit` reads them per hinge. For every hinge of a whole body (rest dihedral <= 55) `lim_h` is exactly 110, bit-identical to today. Measured: rim moves 1 to 10 degrees and the piece is at rest within 1 mm/s on all seven affected pieces.

### 5.4 The neck: latch, depth, centring

`setNeck(plane, t, depth?)`. (1) **Latch:** the first call with `t > 0` computes the rest-space plane once (3.4) and stores it; `neckGoal` and the contact fold band (`softbody.ts:2200-2207`) use it; `setNeck(null, 0)` releases it. (2) **Depth** in [0, 1]: `waist = 1 - depth (1 - NECK_WAIST)`, NECK_WAIST = 0.3 (`softbody.ts:204`); the Gaussian width `NECK_SIGMA` 0.42 and the lobe-swell solve are unchanged. Depth 1 is today's peanut; depth 0.35 is a 0.755 groove. (3) The goal is centred (5.1). **Why depth matters more than anything else the builder does:** the swap replaces the necked parent's rounded, swollen lobes with flat-faced halves, and the size of that jump is the pinch depth. PHYS measured the Chamfer distance (R0, both directions) between the necked parent and the two TRUE halves at the swap, 12 species, one per family:

| waist (depth) | all faces, median / worst | VISIBLE faces only (the new cap faces were interior until the swap), median / worst; species <= 0.08 |
|---|---|---|

### 5.5 `collide` needs four scoped changes (found by running the SHELL's swap flow on true pieces)

Today's `collide` (`softbody.ts:1331-1414`) cannot take flush flat faces, unmatched vertices and piles of 5 to 6 pieces: with the shipped code the swap flow passed every row on 17 of 46 cuts and 23 of 36 nested sequences launched a child above 0.3 m. The four changes apply ONLY to a pair in which at least one body has a cut face (`capFlat`, true for a body built with a region); a pair of whole bodies runs the shipped code and is bit-identical (X21).

1. **Distance and direction from the closest point on the other surface**, not from the nearest vertex's normal (`softbody.ts:1365-1368`: `d = (p - q) . n_q` invents a penetration of the vertex spacing times the sine of the rim angle; 42 to 75 particles were "in contact" at frame 1 with the pieces exactly flush). Closest point on the triangles around the nearest vertex (Ericson 5.1.5; a per-body vertex-to-triangle list built once), `d` signed by the triangle normal, push direction `(p - c)/|p - c|`.
2. **Bounded pushes:** each particle push and each whole-body shove is at most 1 mm per frame (the separation speed is then at most 0.06 m/s per contact).
3. **Skin ramp:** the contact skin of two pieces born in the same step eases in, `skin *= smoothstep(age / 1.0 s)` (a flush pair starts inside its own 32 to 44 mm skin). A ramp of 0.5 s launched a child in 2 of 4 nested sequences, none in 1.0 s.
4. **The whole-body shove** (`CONTACT_RIGID`, `softbody.ts:1395-1399`) is scaled by `min(1, contacts/12)` and is VERTICAL only when the mean contact normal is within 37 degrees of vertical (`|m_y| > 0.8`): a stack is separated up and down, never sideways (one contact moved a whole piece 31 mm sideways; a level-cut upper piece left the lower one and fell 0.55 m).

Measured with the SHELL's flow (8 species, 46 cuts): shipped 17 pass, with (1) to (3) 34, with (1) to (4) 40; nested sequences (12 species x 3): 23 of 36 launch shipped, 0 of 36 with all four. Stacked flat faces rest 39 to 55 mm apart with the contact as PHYS first measured it (E13, before the four changes), **but 26 to 29 mm apart with the final contact of this section** (spec author's re-run, A.6: dollop 26.3 mm, chunkle 28 to 29 mm, whatever the gap at birth, 0 or 50 mm; the contact skin is 32 mm, so the faces rest at 0.82 to 0.89 skins), so the join test treats a gap under `1.1 x` the pair's contact skin (35 to 37 mm) as touching (7.3), which is above the natural spacing, and row X15 gains a measurement that holds that on the port (RT: PR-06, partly rejected: the red team's 1.3 x factor rested on the old 39 to 55 mm); the COM readout of a stacked pair shows +-0.13 to 0.16 m/s that is not motion (it also happens for two whole bodies; the re-run read 130 mm/s with a height jitter of 0.2 to 0.6 mm), so speed gates read POSITIONS.

### 5.6 `planCut`, `prepareCut`, `placeAt`, `meanEdge`

* `planCut(plane, out, mode)`, pure, allocation-free (fills `out`), returns false when the plane misses the body. `mode 'at'`: pull the plane back (3.4), clip `Q` by it (Sutherland-Hodgman per triangle, tetrahedra from a point on the plane): the exact share `fa` of THIS body's volume on side A, the clipped centroids, the second moments (`aspect = sqrt(lambda_min/lambda_max)` of each side's covariance). `'median'`: ignore `plane.point`, bisect `d_r` between the extremes of `Q` along `n_r` (40 steps of a 1280-triangle clip, about 3 ms) **for all 40 steps** (|fa - 0.5| < 1e-9; a stop at 1e-5 left about 3.5 % of five-press runs one press short, A.6; RT: PF5, PR-05). `'latched'`: reuse the latched rest plane and recompute only the live parts (`velA`, `velB`, the frame for placement).
* ~~`cellFa[k]`~~ (removed, RT: PF3, PF17b): a piece is one cell, so the plane either crosses it (then `0 < fa < 1` from the exact clip) or misses it (`planCut` returns false). There is no per-cell classifier and no 1 % sliver that can go to the wrong side of a cut.
* `sound`: for each of the two children, `phi(best centre) >= 0.95` (4.1 step 3); `soundPending` is true when only the cheap centroid test has run and the centre search is the first stage of `prepareCut` (4.5; RT: PF12). `built`: true when both children are cached (`offA`/`offB` are exact then; otherwise estimated from the clipped centroids, error <= 0.04 R0).
* **Prediction = construction:** the child built with `frac = p.frac x fa` has `restVolume` exactly `frac x V3` (1e-12), so the planned share equals the built one. The price: `planCut` clips the REST mesh, not the deformed one on screen. Measured (8 species, vertical plane at 0, 0.2, 0.4 R0): settled 0.0 %; 0.15 s after a nudge 0.0 to 0.6 %; pressed by a finger for 0.6 s -2.3 to +3.9 % of the whole. The Cut tool pauses the other gestures and the shell reads the plan again at the swap with mode `'latched'`, so a pressed squishy cuts at the shares it will be built with.
* `placeAt(at, vel, frame?)`: puts a freshly built piece (before its first step) at world `at` with velocity `vel` and rotation `frame`, exactly (no lift), **and re-homes it: `homeX`, `homeZ` become `at.x`, `at.z`** (they are set only in the constructor from `piece.at`, `softbody.ts:784`, and drive `matCorral` (`:1607`, `:1858`: a piece more than 0.54 m from its home is glided back) and `reset()` (`:815`), so a pre-built piece placed later would otherwise keep the home of its build; RT: PF17a). `meanEdge` publishes the body's mean edge length, so the pair's contact skin `0.25 (meanEdgeA + meanEdgeB)` is known to the shell.
* `FRAC_MIN` drops from 0.125 to 0.124 (`softbody.ts:202`, `:526`, `:1315`) so the exact halves of a 1/4 piece (0.125 +- 2.5e-6) are built as measured. **The same P3 commit re-specifies the one `probe_cut.ts` row that asserts the old value** (X02, line 152: `clamped === 0.125` becomes `clamped === 0.124`), so that file is not red between P3 and P9 (RT: PR-08). `SoftBodyLike.frac` is the body's own measured share (`restVolume / V3`).

### 5.7 `setRegion`, `adoptRest`, `prepareRegion` (the join's physics)

`setRegion(region, seconds)`: the receiver's goal eases from its rest shape `Q` to the target `QT` (the merged piece's rest mesh built at the SAME icosphere detail, so the topology matches vertex by vertex) and its volume target from `V_Q` to `V_T`. In `frameUpdate`, before `GB` is composed, with `e` the smoothstep of the elapsed fraction:

```
QM_i = Q_i + e (QT_i - Q_i)            // re-centre QM with THIS body's M (rule of 5.1)
V*   = V_Q + e (V_T - V_Q)             // the volume target: volScale = V* / restVolume
k    = cbrt(V* / meshVolume(QM));  GB_i = k QM_i     // the goal's own volume equals the constraint's
```
**The rotation is extracted against the goal being morphed.** `A = sum m p q^T` is built against the REST shape `Q` (`softbody.ts:1941-1955`); during a morph the particles chase `QM`, so for a half becoming a whole `A` against the old `Q` is biased, the best-fit rotation drifts and the body wobbles for seconds (measured: receivers still moving at 108 to 241 mm/s one second after, and a swap to a fresh body differed by up to 1.8 R0). The one-line fix is `q = morphOn ? QG : Q` in that loop (the memory arm keeps `Q`): then 1.4 to 9.3 mm/s and differences of 0.004 to 0.10 R0.

At `e = 1` the body COMMITS in place: `adoptRest(rest)` re-runs the rest-dependent part of the constructor (the constructor's rest-dependent block moves into a private `initRest(rest, mesh)`; about 40 arrays are re-allocated: `Q, restLocal, M, invM, Mtot, floppyW, softW, H0, foot, EL, EL0, ESOFT, HD, HD0, HP, HW, H3, HE3, HC, HCH, HSH, LQ, LQG, invH, MEM, QG, QGN, GB, QS, NRM, NN, TPH, S3, SL, SW, ns, volS0, volAlphaT, restVolume, restCenterY, restRadius, params.smOmega`; 40 ms is fine, it is an event), keeps `X, XP, V, GOAL, qr`, clears the morph and publishes a NEW immutable `PieceInfo` object (the old one stays valid). Because the goal at `e = 1` equals the new `Q`, the commit changes no position and no force. A probe row compares the in-place result with a body built fresh from the same region (the swap fallback, which moves particles 0.02 R0 at the median and 0.19 R0 at the worst). **The root is special-cased:** `setRegion([[]], s)` targets `buildRest(genome, mesh_d)` scaled to the volume (not a re-meshed S), so the end state is the original rest shape at that detail.

Measured morphs (12 species x 3 joins): peak centre speed median 0.058, max 0.262 m/s; shape RMS to the target median 0.007, max 0.021 R0; volume 0.998 to 1.006 of the target; centre speed 1 s later median 1.5, max 41 mm/s; no safety reset. **Those joins grew the receiver 2x in volume at most. Reconnect all after five Split-in-two presses grows a piece 4 to 8x (RT: PF4), and there the numbers move:** the red team's runs (12 species) and the spec author's re-run (5 species, `pf4_morph`, A.6) of a detail-2 receiver growing to the detail-2 whole in 1.2 s: **an eighth** peaks at a centre speed of 0.23 to 1.37 m/s (capnap 1.37, of which the legitimate rise of the centre is only 0.46: it hops; chunkle 0.56, boingle 0.42, kneadle 0.37, dollop 0.23), shape RMS 0.006 to 0.041 R0, swap pop (Chamfer to a fresh detail-3 whole) median 0.046; **a quarter** peaks at 0.09 to 0.53 m/s (capnap 0.53, chunkle 0.33), RMS 0.001 to 0.022, pop median 0.036 R0; the same bodies forced to detail 3 give capnap 0.82 (eighth) / 0.48 (quarter), the others 0.10 to 0.59, pop median 0.044 / 0.032. Two consequences: the growing piece must be the LARGEST member of the group (7.2; an eighth is never the receiver of anything), and a flat 0.3 m/s bar on the centre speed mixes the legitimate rise of the centre (it must climb as the body grows: 0.17 to 0.37 m for an eighth, 0 to 0.23 m for a quarter, in 1.2 s) with a hop, so X23 measures the speed in EXCESS of the planned rise (12).

~~`joinable(a, b)` and `regionSound(region)`~~ are removed from the build (RT: PF3): every join target is a single convex cell that was a piece (or the whole) earlier, and both children of every cut were tested sound when the cut was made, so there is nothing left to ask. `prepareRegion(region, budgetMs, maxStages?)` stays: the resumable build of the target at the RECEIVER's detail (a cache hit when the node was built at that detail before; otherwise one ordinary single-cell build of 4 to 40 ms; the prototype's 412 ms were union builds); `setRegion` must not build synchronously inside the join's first frame, so the shell calls it while the join is ARMED (7.3), and a release-join or a slow set-down whose target is not cached waits up to 0.25 s for it with the bridge glowing (RT: PR-12). **`prepareRegion` also allocates the arrays `adoptRest` will swap in** (the ~40 rest-dependent arrays, filled during the morph), so the commit at `e = 1` is pointer swaps and one `PieceInfo`, not an allocation burst; X20 gates `adoptRest` at <= 16 ms. ### 6.1 Entry and prewarm

A swipe becomes the world plane through the camera that contains it, target = the piece the 13 sample rays hit most (unchanged, `cut.ts:493-520`). Picking the Cut tool calls `prewarm()` (a one-line call in `game.ts` `setTool`): one `planCut` of the median vertical plane of the play body and `prepareCut` over three idle frames (pays the JIT and fills the cache with the likely first Split in two), and `stage.warmPieces?.()`. The cold first cut must cost <= 60 ms (X20).

### 6.2 Pipeline (`startCut(p, plane, how)`)

1. `ok = p.body.planCut(plane, plan, 'at')`; false is `miss` ("Nothing to cut there.").
2. Shares: `fa = plan.fa`; `small = min(fa, 1 - fa) x p.frac`; the two absolute shares are `p.frac fa` and `p.frac (1 - fa)`.
3. Refusals, in this order, each with `tremble(0.35)` for 0.28 s and one live-region line, nothing replaced: `small` (`small < PIECE_MIN - PIECE_TOL`, PIECE_MIN 1/8, PIECE_TOL 1e-4); `limit` (`list().length + 1 > MAX_PIECES[quality]`, or the particle rule of 3.2: the cut would make the sixth piece while one of the six is, or would be built as, detail 3 - the live pieces' `vertexCount` is 642, a child is detail 3 when its share is >= 0.35); `thin` (`min(plan.aspectA, plan.aspectB) < ASPECT_MIN`, 0.08, which PHYS may raise after probing slabs; the single-plane minimum measured is 0.172, so in practice `thin` also carries the builder's refusal of 4.1 step 12, which can arrive later, during the neck); `loose` (`!plan.sound`; when `plan.soundPending` the shell starts the neck and a negative answer from `prepareCut` aborts it with this line, 4.5). Why this order: the player fixes `small` by moving the swipe, `limit` by joining, `thin` and `loose` by turning it.
4. First cut only: `pieces = [wholePiece]`; `clearMat()` and its line ("The other squishy went back on the shelf." / "The other N went back on the shelf.") move to the SWAP (6.4 step 4), because the builder can still refuse the cut during the neck (4.1 step 12) and a refused cut must not have put anything back on the shelf (RT: PF2).
5. Allocate the cut: `id = nextId++`, `planes.push({ id, n: plan.rest.n, d: plan.rest.d })`; `cutPiece(p, id)` splits the one leaf `p` into its two kids (3.3; there is no per-cell classifier, RT: PF3). Their regions for PHYS are the kids' literal lists plus the planes those name: one cell each.
6. `anim = { kind: 'neck', ... }`; `audio.cut({ phase: 'start', frac: small, neckS, family, pan, calm, pitch })` exactly as today (`cut.ts:310`).

Nothing in the shell special-cases a feature: a plane through an eye, an ear, a horn, a tail or a petal just leaves a stub on each side (section 4.4).

### 6.3 The neck (kept, shallower) and the pre-build

Every step: `body.setNeck(plane, smooth(t / neckS), depth)` and `stage.setCutSeam(view, planeWorld, k, { from, to })`, where `planeWorld` is recomputed from the LATCHED rest plane (3.4) and `from`/`to` are the two swipe rays evaluated at the target's depth (both lie in the plane); for Split in two, the plane's two ends across the piece's width. `neckS` by family is `cut.ts:43-44` unchanged (0.25 default, 0.3 to 0.4 for the stiff and plastic families); `depth` is the per-family knob of section 11. The swap happens at `neckS + NECK_HOLD_S` (0.08 s; a longer hold creased 12 of 100 bodies at today's depth).

**Pre-build.** Every neck step the shell calls `body.prepareCut(3)` (a 3 ms slice) until it returns true: both children are then built and cached, and the swap frame only takes them. At the swap the shell reads the plan once more with `planCut(plane, plan2, 'latched')` for the live parts only (`velA`, `velB`, the frame) and does NOT re-plan the geometry: the plane is already latched in the rest frame, so the pre-built pieces stay valid however the body wobbled (the SHELL memo's "refresh and maybe rebuild" step is dropped, 17 #2). If `plan2.built` is still false at the swap: extend the hold by at most 0.15 s while `prepareCut(6)` continues; then build in the swap frame (a one-off hitch, logged). If the physics cannot build a piece (the retry ladder of 4.1 step 12 found nothing, or the centre search found the cut unsound) `prepareCut` reports the reason, nothing is replaced, the neck is released (`setNeck(null, 0)`, seam off, pre-built bodies dropped) and the shell says the `thin` or `loose` line; a throw takes the same path with the `thin` line, and the squishy stays as it was; the shell also rolls the tree back (the two kids dropped, `nextId` restored, `cutStack` unchanged) (RT: PF2, PF12). In node and test mode the shell passes a fixed `maxStages` per neck step and `budgetMs = Infinity`, so the swap frame is the same on every machine (4.5; RT: PF14).

### 6.4 The swap (`part`), atomic

1. For each child `c` in {A, B}: `at = parent.center + R(parent.frame) . offset_c` (`plan2.offA/offB`, exact once built, are in the parent's rest-local frame), `vel = vel_c - (v1 - v0)` (`plan2.velA/velB`, the lobe's mean velocity, less the velocity the NECK added to the whole body, `v0` at the neck start and `v1` at the swap: 5.4; RT: PF6), `frame = parent.frame`. The pieces are born exactly FLUSH: cut faces on the plane, gap 0, no overlap.
2. `createBody(g, { piece: { frac, chunk: false, region, frame, at, vel } })` for both, or the pre-built ones with `placeAt(at, vel, frame)`. `piece.frac = body.frac` afterwards (A + B equals the parent's frac to rounding because the physics derives B = parent - A).
3. **One common lift:** read the lowest particle of both placed pieces; if either is below the table, lift BOTH by the same amount (`placeAt` again); never lift one alone (3 to 55 mm of difference overlapped siblings by 22 to 48 particles).
4. `d.beforeChange()` (fingers off); on the first cut `clearMat()` and its line (6.2 step 4); `addExtra(A)`, `addExtra(B)` (`{ shared: true, piece: true, itemId: null }`); the parent, if an extra, `removeExtra`; if the parent is the anchor, `promote(larger child)` (3.5; tie: side A). `bodies.ts:109` stops passing `chunk: true`. Without `stage.setPrimaryBody` (an old stage) the shell falls back to `swapTo` at RENDER's measured cost.
5. `stage.partPieces(ia, ib)`; `audio.cut({ phase: 'separate', frac: min(fA, fB), ... })`; `haptics.poke()`; `say("Cut into N pieces.")`.
6. Start the scripted parting (6.5).

### 6.5 The scripted parting (replaces the closed-loop steering stopgap)

Deleted: `SETTLE_S`, `SETTLE_GAP`, `SETTLE_REACH`, `settling[]`, `steer`, `hold`, `unhold`, `unholdAll` (`cut.ts:63-75`, `:195-226`, `:353-354`). They existed because chunks pushed themselves off their face and were BUILT overlapping by 0.2 to 0.5 m; neither is true any more (a piece stays where it is placed: a piece given 0.22 / 0.6 / 1.2 m/s on the table travels 0.01 to 0.14 m and is back within 0.06 m by 1 s, SHELL M2). What the strands still need is room: a growing gap over about 0.3 s, open loop:

```
G      = PART_GAP[flavour] x (R0 / 0.5) x (calm ? 0.6 : 1)      // metres; table in section 11
PART_S = sticky, slime, putty, mochi 0.45 s; the rest 0.30 s
s      = unit(n.x, 0, n.z)  if |n.y| < 0.75                      // a steep or vertical plane parts sideways
g(t)   = G smoothstep(t / PART_S);   each step: A.moveTo(base_A + 0.5 g s, 3), B.moveTo(base_B - 0.5 g s, 3)   // y = current y; at PART_S moveTo(null)
|n.y| >= 0.75 (a near-level cut): no sliding (stacked flat faces resist it); the UPPER piece is placed G_lift = min(G, 0.05) m above flush and drops (about 1.0 m/s, a soft plop): the plop IS the parting
gravity off: no lift or drop; every cut parts along +-n by g(t)
```
A finger on either piece, a join, the glide home, or a new cut of either piece ends its script at once. Deterministic (sim time only), allocation-free in steady state. Measured (5 species, old pieces): gap = start + G +-15 %, no drift after release, <= 8 mm vertical. The near-level and tilted cases are verified by X13.

**The near-level case is the player's most natural swipe and is untested (RT: PR-13; arithmetic checked by the spec author).** The plane contains the camera ray and the swipe, so at the default pitch 0.27 rad a swipe at screen angle `a` from horizontal has `|n_y| = cos(0.27) cos(a)` = 0.964 cos(a); `|n_y| >= 0.75` holds for `a` within +-38.9 degrees of horizontal, which is **43 % of all swipe orientations, including the left-to-right slice**. For those cuts the parting is the 5 cm plop of the line above: two stacked flat faces with a seam and no gap, and the face is edge-on to the camera that cut it (8.5). Nobody has seen this on real families, so it is a GATE, not a hope: milestone A (15) includes a horizontal swipe on gel, putty, sticky and foam in the owner's eye pass. If the plop reads as "nothing happened", the first lever is tuning item **T12, the ledge**: the upper piece also moves `0.35 g(t)` horizontally along the down-slope direction of the cut plane (any horizontal direction perpendicular to the swipe for an exactly level plane), so the new face shows as a ledge; it is a scripted piece motion, so it works in Calm. The red team's second lever, a small outward yaw of each piece (a "door opening", 6 to 8 degrees), is NOT built by default: it needs a kinematic turn that `moveTo` does not offer and its effect is unmeasured (tuning item T13). The optional camera peek (owner question 3) stays the other lever, and is off in Calm.

### 6.6 Split in two (fixes VERIFY_SHELL4_A F1: it stalled at 4 pieces; RT: PR-02, PR-05, PF5)

`splitInTwo()`: target = the LARGEST piece (ties: lowest index, anchor first, then creation order). If `target.frac < 2 PIECE_MIN - 2 PIECE_TOL` (= 0.2498; the first version used `- 1e-6`, a tighter tolerance than the `small` refusal it guards, and the median search stopped at 1e-5, so 3.5 % to 3.7 % of five-press runs stalled at five pieces: A.6. `planCut` now bisects to 1e-9 and the guard has the same tolerance as the refusal), `canSplit` is false and the line is "No piece is big enough to cut again. Undo the last cut or use Reconnect all." (the old line "Swipe near an edge to peel off a small one" is false advice in that state: a piece under 1/4 cannot give two parts of 1/8; "join two" is a gesture a keyboard player does not have). Otherwise the plane depends on what is being cut:

* **The first press on a WHOLE squishy cuts along the squishy's OWN line between the eyes, whatever the camera shows (the owner's example; RT: PR-02).** The normal is the squishy's rest +x axis in the world, `n = R(frame) (1, 0, 0)`, the axis the two eyes are mirrored about (`face.ts:122-125`: azimuth +-(9 + 17 spacing) degrees about +z). The plane passes through the midpoint `m` of the two eye centres when `m` is known: `stage.eyeAnchors?.(viewId)` returns the two centres in the rest-local frame of the whole (new optional stage member, section 10), the point is `center + R(frame) m`, and the shell uses that plane when its smaller share is in [0.40, 0.60] (one `planCut(..., 'at')` reads it); otherwise, or when the stage offers no `eyeAnchors`, the plane is the `'median'` plane along the same normal. Why: the first version cut "as the camera sees it", and every eye sits at an azimuth of only 9 to 26 degrees from the front, so rotating the plane by `psi` moves it `0.9 sin(psi)` R0 at the eyes, which is the whole clearance. The red team measured (and the spec author re-ran, A.6) the median plane of the camera-relative family on 50 species: eyes on opposite pieces for 50 at 0 degrees of camera yaw, 49 at 2.5 to 10, 47 at 12.5, 45 at 15, **2 at 20 and 0 from 30 to 90 degrees**; with the rest-size bead also clear of the plane: 45, 43, 37, 19, **5 at 10 degrees**, 1 at 12.5, 0 at 15. Ten degrees is 23 px of drag (`feel.ts:38`, 0.0075 rad/px), a touch player orbits with any empty-space drag, and nothing recentres the camera (`stage.ts:512`). The eye-midline plane does not depend on the camera at all, and the squishy itself turns only 0 to 2.3 degrees in 4 s of one-finger play (red team `yaw_drift.ts`, 5 species). Owner question 4 is answered this way for the FIRST press; a swipe stays what-you-draw; the camera-relative rule below stays for every later press.
* **Later presses and any press on a piece:** `n0 = cross(view ray to the piece's centre, camera up)` (`cut.ts:530-536`) projected onto the horizontal plane and normalised, then four vertical candidates `n_k = rotate(n0, k x 45 degrees about +y)`, k = 0..3, each `planCut(..., 'median')`, and the k that maximises `min(aspectA, aspectB)` (prefer the smaller k unless the best beats it by more than 0.03). `fa` is 0.5 to 1e-9, so both parts equal `target.frac / 2`: never `small` while `target.frac >= 2 PIECE_MIN - 2 PIECE_TOL`, never `thin` on the 50 shapes.

The sides and the rest of the pipeline are those of a swipe. `canSplit = list().length < max() && list().some(p => p.frac >= 2 PIECE_MIN - 2 PIECE_TOL)`. A unit row asserts that a 0.249985 piece can be split.

Measured (SHELL M3, exact nested regions on the 50 rest shapes): five presses reach six pieces on 50 of 50 species, smallest piece exactly 1/8, worst aspect min 0.15, median 0.28 (a repeated parallel cut makes pancakes of aspect 0.05, which is why the candidates exist). The first press through the centre of mass is NOT a halving plane (+x share 0.471 to 0.532), so the median plane is used when the eye-midline share falls outside [0.40, 0.60]. **A first press at any share from 0.35 to 0.65 still reaches six pieces in five presses with every piece >= 0.1375** (red team `first_press_share.mjs`), so the unequal first press costs nothing, and it removes the zero margin the exact halves had. For the eye-midline plane the red team found the rest-size bead clear on 48 of 50 species (granulo -0.018 and marigel -0.022 R0 still cross: their eyes sit 0.15 R0 from the centre line) and the squeezed reach (x1.24) clear on 46 (munchip, granulo, petalop, marigel cross), shares 0.421 (wrigglo) to 0.579. ## 7. Reconnect

### 7.1 What the player does

Joining is a **Hand-tool gesture** (RT: PR-01). With the Cut tool in hand a press draws the blade and returns (`game.ts:623`; the press never reaches `gestures.pointerDown`, so no grab, no `driver.touch.contact()` and no `dragOf`), and the join machine of 7.3 is fed only by those, so the Cut tool cannot join and the harness presses `c` to leave it before every join gesture (`browser_shell.mjs:1782`). Drag a piece onto another and hold: as they come close the cut faces that belong together light up and the live region says how many pieces are coming ("Joining 3 pieces."), and after about 0.4 s of touching they flow into one piece. Let go of a dragged piece while it touches another, or set a carried piece down gently on another, and it joins at once. Poking, squeezing, rubbing, pulling away, tossing and bumping pieces together never joins them. Any two pieces can join. Whole again is exactly the squishy you started with. The game tells the player how in three places: the Cut bar ("With the Hand, drag a piece onto another and hold to join them."), a one-time hint when the tool goes back to the Hand with two or more pieces (9.3), and the live-region line while a join is armed.

### 7.2 Which pieces join, and what the merged piece is (the rule the owner asked for)

Given pieces P and Q (two leaves of the cut tree, 3.3):

1. **GROUP.** `N = lca(P, Q)`; the group is every piece under N (each leaf under N is a live piece). The merged piece is N's cell, which was a piece (or the whole) earlier, so it is a valid body: both of its kids were tested sound when the cut that made them was planned, and its own mesh passed the gate of 4.1 step 12 when it was a piece (a node last built at another detail is built once more, while the join is ARMED, by `prepareRegion`). **Twins** (P and Q are the two kids of N): the group is the two; the cut between them disappears and the merged cell is the parent's, literal for literal. **Anyone else:** the group is everything that was cut from N ("Joined 3 pieces into one."). Examples: after a cut into A and B and a second cut of B into B1 and B2, joining B1 and B2 gives B; joining A and B1 gives A, B1 and B2, which is the whole.
2. **ROOT.** When N is the root the squishy is whole: the last animation arrives and the shell swaps in a fresh `createBody(genome)` (7.5). This holds for ANY sequence of cuts and joins in ANY order (property test X18): the join of the last two groups has the root as its common ancestor.
3. **Receiver.** The body that survives and morphs is the group's LARGEST member by `frac` (ties: detail 3 before detail 2, then the anchor, then the lower index; RT: PF4). If it is not the anchor, the anchor flows in as a giver and `promote(receiver)` makes the receiver the play body when the flow ends (identity unchanged). Rationale: the receiver grows by the givers' shares, and the red team's runs show that growing a SMALL piece is the dangerous case (an eighth growing to the whole in 1.2 s hopped at 1.37 m/s on capnap, 5.7); the first version made the anchor the receiver, and after five Split-in-two presses the anchor is a 0.125 or 0.25 piece. With the largest member the growth is at most 6x (the group is all six pieces and the largest is at least 1/6). A merged piece keeps its receiver's mesh detail (5.7); it is promoted to detail 3 only at `swapWhole` (T8, 7.5).
4. **Always valid, always ends whole.** Every group is a node of the tree (a convex cell), its volume is the exact sum of its members, and joining everything in any order ends at the root (property test X18: 100 000 random operation sequences).

What this rule costs (measured by the spec author on the red team's own tree generator, 50 species, one tree each, 10 trees of five pieces and 40 of six, every pair of pieces; A.6): over ALL 700 pairs the group is exactly the two pieces for 14.6 %, three for 14.3 %, four for 13.9 %, five for 14.0 % and six for 43.3 %; the whole squishy comes back for 50.4 % of pairs. For pairs that really TOUCH across a cut face when they are cut (512 of 700, 73 %; contact area at least 0.02 R0^2 on the cut plane, sampled), the group is exactly the pair for 19.9 % (twins), three pieces for 17.4 %, four for 13.9 %, five for 13.5 % and six for 35.4 %; the whole squishy comes back for 43.0 % of touching pairs and 53.7 % of touching non-twins. The first version predicted that two touching non-twins would join as just those two (a "FIT" union); its builder measured IoU 0.84 median, 0.42 minimum and 39 % reverted rims on such unions (3.3), so the safe rule ships and the cost is stated to the owner (17.2 question 1). Mitigations that are built: the ARMED glow and the live-region line ("Joining 3 pieces.") say what is about to happen before it does; Undo cut steps back one cut at a time; Reconnect all remains.

### 7.3 The join gesture (the intent machine; one tracked pair; only when the tool is the HAND, there are two or more pieces and no animation; no allocation per step)

The gesture is CUT.md's own and keeps working as the fix round verified it (VERIFY_SHELL4_A_R1: a piece dragged into its twin and let go while they touch joins at once; a tap, press, squeeze, rub or pull away does not). CUT2 adds the facing-face glow, the group logic of 7.2, the release-speed gate and a contact distance tied to the contact skin:

```
inputs  P = the piece the finger is on; touching() = a finger or grab on it; grab = the real finger's grab and its hand displacement (driver.touch.dragOf);
        carried = P.body.metrics.carried; v = |horizontal speed of P's centre| over 3 steps; gap(P, Q) = skin gap along the line of centres (cut.ts:197-203)
tool    the machine runs only while the tool is the Hand; a press in the Cut tool is a blade and never starts, advances or completes a join (row X25)
intent(P -> Q) = carried  OR  ( grab AND  hand moved toward Q by >= DRAG_TOWARD_K (0.25) x radius(P) )       // the hand's requested position, as today
NONE    -> ARMED    when intent and gap < ARM_REACH (0.25 R_small, about 10 cm)    show: setBridge(P, Q, 0.15); the facing cut faces of the whole group glow 0.35;
                                                                                    say "Joining K pieces." once per arm (K = the group's size, 7.2)
ARMED   -> HOLDING  when gap < TOUCH_GAP                                           timer starts; bridge 0.15 -> 0.55 and the glow 0.35 -> 0.8 over JOIN_HOLD_S
HOLDING -> JOIN     when held >= JOIN_HOLD_S (0.4 s) with the finger still down   (latched while touching() and gap < TOUCH_KEEP_GAP: soft bodies rebound a few cm)
ARMED, HOLDING -> NONE when intent has been false for > 0.25 s, or gap > TOUCH_KEEP_GAP
RELEASE (the step where touching() turns false), by the constant RELEASE_JOIN:
   'touching' (DEFAULT = CUT.md and the fix-round-1 behaviour, as the shipped code does it, cut.ts:610-631): JOIN at once if the contact STARTED during this drag
              (state HOLDING was reached at some step: gap < TOUCH_GAP while dragging toward Q), gap < TOUCH_KEEP_GAP now, and the release speed v < CARRY_SETDOWN_V (0.8 m/s)
              (merely being ARMED, within about 10 cm after a stretch toward Q, is NOT enough: that is stretch-and-release play, which pays pull SP and must not join; RT: PR-06)
   'carried'  (owner question 2, option B): JOIN at once only if P was `carried` within the last 0.1 s, v < 0.8 m/s and gap < TOUCH_KEEP_GAP; a plain drag released never joins
a throw (v >= 0.8 m/s) never joins; a physics 'bump' is not an input
TOUCH_GAP      = max(0.03, 1.1 x pairSkin) m,  pairSkin = 0.25 (meanEdgeA + meanEdgeB);  TOUCH_KEEP_GAP = TOUCH_GAP + 0.06 m
```
Why `TOUCH_GAP` is tied to the contact skin and what it must be above: it has to exceed the gap at which a flush pair rests, or a pair at its natural spacing could never be reached by a drag without pressing. The first version quoted 39 to 55 mm (PHYS E13, the contact before its four changes) and the red team asked for 1.3 x the skin; the spec author's re-run with the contact of 5.5 gives **26 to 29 mm** (A.6), 0.82 to 0.89 of the 32 mm skin, so `1.1 x` (35 to 37 mm) is already above it, and X15 gains a row that measures the resting gap of flush pairs on the port and asserts it is under `TOUCH_GAP` (50 species). If the port's contact rests higher, the factor rises to meet the measurement (RT: PR-06). The release-speed gate is new: a flick against the neighbour is a toss, not a join (the SHELL memo reasoned from the code that the old rule could join on a toss; X15 measures it). What the default cannot tell apart is "dragged into its twin" from "stretched a lobe toward it and let go after touching it": the grab pulls a lobe, so both are the same physical act. Pieces now rest flush, so the chance that a stretch-and-release ends touching is higher than with the old chunks; the switch to `'carried'` is one constant and is owner question 2 (recommendation: ship `'touching'`, flip it if the first playtest shows accidental joins). Under both settings a tap, press, squeeze, rub, pull away, **stretch toward the neighbour and let go at 6 to 10 cm without touching it**, toss and bump never join, and a piece resting beside its twin at any distance never joins by itself (X15).

While ARMED the shell computes the group once (7.2, a tree walk), says its size, and calls `prepareRegion(nodeRegion, 3)` every step, so the target is built (or found in the cache) by the time the join starts and the join's first frame never pays a build.

### 7.4 The flow

* Receiver `setRegion(merged, JOIN_S)` (0.8 s; `JOIN_ALL_S` 1.2 s when the group is the whole): the shape and volume morph (5.7). Every giver: `setFrac(PIECE_MIN, JOIN_S)` and `setGhost(giver, true)` (a ghost has no contact push, which would shove the receiver off) and flows along the existing path (`flowOf`, `flowD`, `follow`: first to a held surface gap of `BRIDGE_GAP_K` 0.3 of the smaller radius for `BRIDGE_HOLD_S` 0.3 s while the glowing neck is on show, then in), toward the group's volume-weighted meeting point: `follow()` also moves the receiver by `w = f_givers / f_group` of the distance (`moveTo` stiffness 3), so the result stands where the pieces were, not where the receiver happened to be.
* The visual language is CUT.md's own: "they bridge with a glowing neck, flow together and become one bigger piece, a gloopy merge sound". The existing `setBridge` tube stays; the facing cut faces glowing is the addition; the render of the morph and the commit is 8.8. (RENDER's "knit mode", pieces meeting face to face with no tube and a swap on touch, is NOT built: it needs the giver's yaw aligned to the receiver's, which nothing in the physics provides, 17 #7.) Tuning item T7: choose the held-gap point on the side of the shared cut face when the pieces are twins.
* End: givers removed (`removeExtra`), `recv.frac = body.frac`, `rejoin({ frac: recv.frac, ... })` once, `haptics.release()`, "Joined. Now N pieces." / "Joined K pieces into one.". The commit is in place (`adoptRest`: pointer swaps of arrays prepared during the morph, 5.7), the receiver publishes a NEW `PieceInfo` and RENDER rebuilds that view's piece mesh once (<= 8 ms) keeping its id, clock and face state; the cap look of the vanished faces has already faded to skin (8.8).

### 7.5 The last join, and "bit-for-bit"

When the group is the root: after the flow, a piece further than `HOME_NEAR` (0.06 m) from the middle glides there first (`moveTo`, at most `HOME_MAX_S` 1.6 s; a whole body built off the middle keeps that spot as its mat home), then `swapWhole`: `createBody(genome)` (no `piece`, the original constructor path and the original genome object), `swapTo({ body, itemId, nickname, silent: true })` (the one place `swapTo` stays: nothing but the survivor is left to rebuild). Whole means: same genome object, `restLocal` hash equal to a never-cut body's, `frac` 1, volume within 0.5 %, `piece` undefined (today's whole-body render path, so the picture is the same code), at the middle (abs(x), abs(z) <= 1e-9). The morph's end state is within RMS 0.007 R0 median / 0.021 max of that rest shape for a 2x growth and 0.009 / 0.022 for a quarter becoming the whole, and swapping to the fresh body moves particles 0.02 R0 median / 0.19 R0 worst in PHYS's prototype. **The first version's swap-pop bar (median <= 0.03 R0, p95 <= 0.12) was set from the half-to-whole case at detail 3; the measured pop of a quarter becoming the whole is median 0.036 (detail 2) / 0.032 (detail 3), p95 0.045 / 0.039, max 0.057 / 0.048 R0, and of an eighth 0.046 / 0.044 (A.6), mostly the morph's residual and the yaw, not the mesh detail (RT: PF4).** X04 therefore bounds the pop at median <= 0.05 R0 and p95 <= 0.10 R0 for a receiver of 1/6 and up, the film strip judges it, and a visible pop is answered by RENDER's 0.15 s cross-fade of the final view swap (T14), not by a looser number. The yaw at the swap is reported (a fresh body is built at yaw 0; a tossed, spinning receiver pops by its yaw; if the median exceeds 5 degrees the glide gains a turn, risk K9). Tuning item **T8**: if X23 (12) fails on 2 or more of 12 species for a detail-2 receiver at the restated bar, the receiver is rebuilt at detail 3 at the start of the join (a new body at the same pose from the cached detail-3 build; with the forced-detail-3 runs of A.6 the capnap hop falls from 1.37 to 0.82 m/s for an eighth and 0.53 to 0.48 for a quarter); not built by default because the largest-member rule removes the case it fixes.

### 7.6 Reconnect all, Undo cut, leave paths, keyboard

Reconnect all (button, 1.2 s) is the same machine with the group = every piece: flows planned from where everything stands now, receiver per 7.2, `setRegion([[]], JOIN_ALL_S)`, then 7.5. `rejoin({ frac: 1, all: true })`, "Whole again.". With Skip animations it is instant and the button says "Whole again." itself. **Undo cut** (owner question 5; RT: PR-14) is the same machine with the group = the two kids of the top of `cutStack` (always an exact twin join, 2 pieces), say "Cut undone. Now N pieces."; it is a button beside Split in two and Reconnect all in the popover, disabled when there is nothing to undo. **Leave paths** (CUT.md rule 2) are untouched: `leaving()` = `reconnectAll(false)` (no animation, `finishAll(false)` removes every other piece and `swapWhole`); `abortAnim` unwinds a neck (`setNeck(null, 0)`, seam off, pre-built bodies dropped) or a join (ghost off). The Hoard never lists a piece. A keyboard player pokes only the anchor and cannot join two chosen pieces: the accepted limit of CUT.md (2 rule 9), softened by Undo cut and Reconnect all.

## 8. Render (RENDER; `src/render`)

### 8.1 What the render lane relies on in the rest mesh (invariants I1 to I7 for PHYS)

I1 closed outward mesh on today's icosphere topology, star-shaped about `rayOrigin`, `restLocal` mass-centred to 1e-6 R0. I2 an exact flat face (every cap vertex within 1e-6 R0 of its plane; the cap is a union of WHOLE triangles) with a clean rim (the rim is an edge loop on both the plane and the silhouette): a plain radial clip leaves 70 to 90 triangles per cut that mix cap and skin vertices, a 0.076 to 0.093 R0 bevel (about 22 px on a 250 px body) that reads as a rounded edge, which is exactly what the owner does not want. I3 no sliver cap faces (see the restated bar in 13.1). I4 particles, goal and edge rest lengths agree at t = 0. I5 `piece.whole` arrays are shared by identity; `frac` is the true fraction; plane ids are stable for the life of the cut. I6 pieces are born flush in the whole's pose (the minimum form: the parent's frame; the preferred form, initialising particles from the deformed whole, is not designed). I7 a merge changes `piece` by identity: PHYS updates the rest arrays in place and publishes a NEW immutable `PieceInfo` in the same step; the view compares `inner.piece` by reference each frame and rebuilds once. **The RENDER memo's sentence "until then the old classification stands, which is right during a join" was written for its "knit mode" (faces meet, swap on touch), which this spec does not build (17 #7); here the receiver morphs its rest shape for 0.8 s BEFORE the commit, so the old classification would stay on a bulging surface for the whole morph. It is not acceptable: 8.8 (RT: PR-03).** A body with `piece` undefined or `cells = [[]]` takes today's code path unchanged.

### 8.2 The fine mesh of a piece

The geodesic of today (`jelly.ts:247-258`, frequency 32 / 24 / 16) cannot make a clean edge: casting directions onto a flat face stretches the fine vertices toward the rim (2.1 to 2.3 % of fine triangles straddle it, edge across the rim 0.07 to 0.08 R0, up to 0.175). A piece's fine mesh is built by **subdividing the sim triangles n x n** (no ray casting): for each sim triangle and lattice point `(i, j)`, `i + j <= n`, barycentric `((n-i-j)/n, i/n, j/n)`; vertices on a shared sim edge or corner are shared. **Class split:** a sim triangle is `cap k` when its three vertices are on plane k, else `skin`; fine vertices are welded separately per class, so a rim vertex exists TWICE at the same position and the index buffer is open exactly along the crease: a mathematically crisp edge, no threshold, no blur. Attributes: `aCap = (k, rim)`, `aCapP` = W-position / `whole.radius` (the cut face's noise coordinate, the SAME on both faces of a cut), `aCapD` = depth of the piece behind the point (a thickness map: splat skin vertices into an 8 x 8 grid in the face's plane, max per cell, one smoothing pass, read bilinearly; about 0.1 ms; the exact ray version measured 30 ms), `aRest = normalize(P_W - m_W)` with `m_W` the whole mesh's vertex mean (so patterns, Epic two-tone, Legendary aurora and Mythic thin film continue across the cut with no other change; tolerance `|Delta aRest| <= 0.008`).

**Per frame:** skin normals from skin triangles only, face normals from cap triangles only (the same two relax passes restricted to each list), so a rim vertex has a skin normal that knows nothing of the face and a face normal that knows nothing of the skin; a flat face has equal corner normals, so Phong tessellation adds nothing and the face stays planar, while a dented face bends smoothly. New outputs: `jelly.caps[k]` (centroid, mean unit normal, d of each live cut face; these are the eye clip planes, the core clip planes and the strand anchors); extents and `press`/`pull` use `0.45 x whole.radius` for a piece (a thin slab has a tiny restRadius and would over-report dents). **Idle skip (pieces only; wholes stay bit-identical):** a piece whose sim positions moved less than 2e-5 R0 since the last frame it built skips normals, extents and the fine loop (the eyes still animate). If PHYS ever delivers a rest mesh that violates I2, the builder flags `degraded` and falls back to the same subdivision with a per-vertex majority class (a chamfer of one sim triangle); the probe reports `rimClean`, so it is never silent.

**Size per piece** (the "6 pieces cost no more than 2 whole bodies" bar): `n = max(2, floor(sqrt(2 N_tier frac^(2/3) / T) + 0.35))` with `N_tier` = 10242 / 5762 / 2562 fine vertices (high / med / low), `T` = 1280 sim triangles (320 for a detail-2 piece). Over every partition of the whole into 1 to 6 pieces (each >= 1/8, steps of 1/64) the worst total is 1.923 (high), 1.890 (med) and 2.501 (low, capped at 4 pieces; at low the floor `n >= 2` makes four pieces 6408 vertices, still under one high-tier body) times one whole body's fine vertices; without scaling it is 6.0. The CPU model puts six MOVING pieces at about 1.0 of two whole bodies (the 0.45 ms of sim-normal work does not shrink with the piece), so the idle skip is the margin, not an option. **The `low` figure is computed for at most four pieces, but the auto governor (`quality.ts:67`: a 1 s average over 24 ms drops a tier) can drop med to low AFTER six pieces exist, and a quality drop below the piece count only blocks new cuts (SHELL 5.1), so five and six pieces can render at low (RT: PR-12).** RP8 computes the 5- and 6-piece totals at low as well; bar <= 2.6 x one whole body's fine vertices at low; if a state exceeds it, the views of pieces under 1/4 use `n = 2` and the next tier's `fineFreq`.

### 8.3 Who holds what

Every position-bound thing is first defined in W and then handed to the piece that contains it. A W-point P belongs to a piece iff it lies inside one of its cells, `all_k (n_k . P - d_k < 0)`, a point exactly on a plane going to the side whose outward normal is lexicographically larger; that partitions every point to exactly one piece, so specks and stars are conserved to the unit.

* **Eyes** (the face is two eyes; there is no mouth, `face.ts:1`, `:10`; the genome has `eyeStyle, eyeSpacing, eyeSize, eyeHeight`). Placement stays the function of the whole, computed ONCE per squishy on a `WholeRef` (keyed by the identity of `piece.whole.restLocal`) with the exact formulas of `face.ts:105` (`radius = restRadius (0.095 + 0.07 eyeSize)`), `:122-125` (elevation `3 + 26 eyeHeight` degrees, azimuth `max(9 + 17 eyeSpacing degrees, azMin)`) and `:161-163` (the direction cast against the rest mesh), but with `whole.radius`, so eye size no longer shrinks with a piece. **Rule: clip, exactly at the cut face.** Per piece and eye: `rho = r x 1.24 x max(sx, sy)` (the widest the bead gets); `s = max_k (n_k . P_e - d_k)` over the planes of the cell holding P_e; remaining width `f = (rho - s) / (2 rho)` clamped to [0, 1]; **keep iff `f >= 0.12`** (both pieces satisfy `f_A + f_B = 1`; the lost sliver exists only while they are apart). A kept eye gets 1 to 2 clip planes in world space, refreshed each frame from `jelly.caps[k]` (the actual deformed face, so a dented face still clips flush): `material.clippingPlanes` on the dome and arc materials with `renderer.localClippingEnabled = true` (currently never set: no hit for `localClippingEnabled` or `clippingPlanes` in `src/`); the eye materials are created WITH two inert planes (normal 0, constant 1e6) on every Face, so wholes and pieces share one program (no first-cut compile); the dome becomes `DoubleSide` with a `gl_FrontFacing` branch that returns flat ink-dark, so where the front faces are clipped away you see the solid bead's cross-section. Pose: anchor `h` = the nearest skin point of the piece to the eye; `off = q_e - h` set to 0 when `|off| < 0.01 R0` (so unclipped eyes are bit-for-bit today's pose), applied each frame; footprint samples beyond a cut face use the one-sided difference. Blink timer salt = a hash of the piece's first plane id (so two halves do not blink in lockstep; a whole or merged body takes salt 0 = today); squint and widen follow the PIECE's own compression. **Why clip and not "the piece with the centre keeps the whole eye":** it is the only rule under which two pieces recombine to the identical image, and the other makes a bead overhang the face by up to 0.12 to 0.27 R0 (floating in air). **Fallback ladder** (decided by render probe RP2): (a) clip; (b) if the `DoubleSide` + clipping + transparent path proves unreliable in the browser harness, an eye whose remaining width is under 50 % is hidden and one at or over 50 % is kept whole and shifted inward by `rho - s` so it never overhangs; (c) hide it. An eye is within its reach of a random swipe plane in 46.3 % of cuts (35 to 54 % by species, RENDER S3), so this path is the common case, not an edge case.
* **A piece with no eye** (79 % of random cuts leave one) gets no `Face` object: no eye materials, no meshes, no blink, no mood, four fewer draw calls. It is jelly: it still dents, blushes, glows under a fingertip, spawns bubbles, shows its glitter, pattern, iridescence, core share, cut face, rim light, aura and light pool. The analytic core halo keeps a floor `coreAmt = max(share, 0.35)` (a piece without ember is lit by its neighbour's); the blob itself appears only if the piece holds ember.
* **Core.** A sphere at W (0, 0, 0), radius `rc = 0.17 x scale_W x coreSize`, halo 3.4 rc. In the piece it sits at `-offset` (new `Core.offset`, zero for wholes). Kept fraction per plane: 1 for `s >= rc`, 0 for `s <= -rc`, else `1 - (rc - s)^2 (2 rc + s) / (4 rc^3)` (spherical cap), the product for two planes within `rc`. The blob gets two world clip planes (from `jelly.caps`) so it never pokes out of the face; the camera-facing halo billboard is not clipped, it is scaled by the kept fraction. The jelly's analytic halo follows `uCoreWorld` with no other change.
* **Glitter and constellation stars.** `Glitter.layout` replays the whole body's seeded stream against the WHOLE's mapper, keeps the specks inside the piece's cells and re-locates them (a ray from `rayOrigin` against the piece mesh; the `nearest()` brute force, 72 to 87 us, for non-star shapes). Total specks over pieces = the whole's, exactly, each in exactly one piece; a star link is drawn only when both stars are on the piece.
* **Colour and time.** One genome gives one `JellyMaterials` per view with identical uniforms, so white balance, absorption and tier tint are identical across pieces; a new piece view inherits the `clock` of the view it was cut from (within 0.25 s, same genome seed), so aurora, thin film, core pulse and glitter twinkle do not restart.

### 8.4 The cut face

New uniforms per view (`uCapRough, uCapCoat, uCapTint, uCapPore, uCapPoreF, uCapWet, uCapGlow, uCapDepth` family constants; `uFresh, uFreshIdx`; `uCoreR`), program key bumped. ### 8.5 Seam, strands, bridge, fresh sheen, calm

### 8.8 The join commit (RT: PR-03; RENDER task R3b, 1 to 1.5 days)

* **`SoftBodyLike.morphT?: number`** (0..1, the `e` of 5.7; 0 when no morph runs), published by the receiver (section 10). Givers need nothing new: their `frac` already falls with `setFrac`.

* **New features fade in, old features shrink with their host.** The receiver's new eye, specks, stars and core share (the ones the membership rule of 8.3 will hand it at the commit) fade in over `morphT` 0.6 to 1 (eyes through `Face.alpha`; specks by index; the core halo by its kept fraction). * **One frame, no step.** After the cross-fade the classification swap at the commit is invisible; for Reconnect all the same fade covers the last 0.3 s before `swapWhole`, and the swap pop of 7.5 is judged on the film strip (`cut2_reassemble_ab`); if a pop shows, RENDER cross-fades the final view swap over 0.15 s (tuning item T14).

## 10. Contract (`src/contracts.ts`; every member optional and additive; ONE editor at the freeze: the orchestrator, task O1)

This block is the reconciled whole, revised by the red-team fold: **no `joinable`, no `regionSound`, no `cellFa`/`cellN`** (every piece is one cell, 3.3), plus `soundPending`, `morphT`, `eyeAnchors`, and a `maxStages` argument. /* ── CUT2 (TRUE CUT, _spec/CUT2.md): a piece is the real part of the squishy on its side of the cut planes ── */
export interface Quat { x: number; y: number; z: number; w: number }

/** A plane in the WHOLE squishy's rest frame W (origin = the whole's rest centre of mass, axes = its rest axes, metres). Side A: n . p >= d. Shell -> PHYS. */
export interface RestPlane { id: number; n: V3; d: number }
/** Which part of the whole a body is, as the shell says it: a union of convex cells; a cell is a list of signed plane ids (+id side A, -id side B) whose
 *  intersection, with the species silhouette, is that cell. cells [[]] (or no region) = the whole. `planes` holds every plane the cells name.
 *  THIS BUILD asks for exactly ONE cell (a node of the cut tree); the list is kept so a union builder (tuning item T9) needs no contract change. */
export interface PieceRegion { planes: RestPlane[]; cells: number[][] }
/** PHYS -> RENDER: one bounding plane of a cell, in W; the piece lies where dot(n, p) <= d; n is a UNIT vector pointing OUT of the piece. The two faces of one cut carry
 *  the same id with opposite n and d. A literal +id (side A of RestPlane k) is { n: -n_k, d: -d_k, id }; -id is { n: n_k, d: d_k, id }. */
export interface CutPlaneRest { n: V3; d: number; id: number }
export interface PieceInfo {
  /** The uncut squishy's DETAIL-3 rest mesh (the same values as `new SoftBody(genome).restLocal`), the SAME array objects for every piece of one squishy
   *  (pieces are grouped by this identity). `radius` = its restRadius. NOT the physics' internal detail-4 source solid. */
  readonly whole: { readonly restLocal: Float32Array; readonly indices: Uint32Array; readonly radius: number };
  /** restLocal_piece[i] = (the same material point in W) - offset. Composes for nested cuts. */
  readonly offset: V3;
  /** Piece coordinates: the centre the piece was re-meshed from; the mesh is star-shaped about it. */
  readonly rayOrigin: V3;
  /** The piece is the union of these convex cells (ONE cell in this build); each cell is the intersection of its planes. A fresh whole has [[]]. A merge publishes a NEW immutable PieceInfo. */
  readonly cells: ReadonlyArray<ReadonlyArray<CutPlaneRest>>;
}

export interface PieceOpts {
  /* existing: frac, chunk (now optional), cutNormal, at, vel.  With `region`, `chunk` and `cutNormal` are ignored and `at` is EXACT (the constructor does not lift the piece). */
  chunk?: boolean;            // was required; the shell no longer passes it (RT: PR-15)
  region?: PieceRegion;       // absent = the old build (a whole at `at`, or a legacy chunk)
  frame?: Quat;               // the parent's best-fit rotation: the child is born in the parent's pose (particles rotated about the parent's centre, qr set)
}

export interface CutPlan {
  rest: { n: V3; d: number };            // the plane in W (normal toward side A); the physics latches it. prepareCut's retry ladder (4.1 step 12) may shift d by at most 0.012 R0 and then updates every field below in the same step
  fa: number;                            // share of THIS body's volume on side A, from the REST clip (= what is built, to 1e-12)
  offA: V3; offB: V3;                    // each child's rest origin in THIS body's rest-local frame: world = center + R(frame) . off
  velA: V3; velB: V3;                    // mean world velocity of each half of the current mesh (the shell subtracts the velocity the neck added, 5.4)
  aspectA: number; aspectB: number;      // sqrt(smallest / largest covariance eigenvalue) of each child, 0..1 (1 = ball, 0.1 = pancake)
  sound: boolean;                        // false: a child is not one body (the cut would split off two separate parts)
  soundPending?: boolean;                // true: only the cheap centroid test has run (sound is provisional); the centre search is the first stage of prepareCut (4.5)
  built: boolean;                        // true: both children are cached and offA/offB are exact (otherwise estimated from the clipped centroids, <= 0.04 R0)
}

export interface SoftBodyLike {
  /* existing CUT members stay (frac, measureCut, setNeck, setFrac, collide); `frac` is now the body's own measured share (restVolume / V3). */
  readonly piece?: PieceInfo;                                       // absent on a whole squishy
  readonly meanEdge?: number;                                       // mean edge length (m): the pair's contact skin is 0.25 (meanEdgeA + meanEdgeB)
  readonly morphT?: number;                                         // 0..1: progress of a running setRegion morph (the e of 5.7), 0 otherwise; the render cross-fades the old cut faces to skin by it (8.8)
  /** Pure; fills `out`, no allocation, <= 4 ms. 'at': the plane as given. 'median': ignore plane.point, find the offset along plane.normal that halves the volume (all 40 steps, 1e-9).
   *  'latched': reuse the rest plane latched by setNeck and recompute only velA, velB. false when the plane misses the body. */
  planCut?(plane: CutPlane, out: CutPlan, mode?: 'at' | 'median' | 'latched'): boolean;
  /** After a successful planCut: build the two children in time slices (call once per neck step, 3 ms); true when both are cached, false while stages remain,
   *  'thin' | 'loose' when the cut is refused (the retry ladder found no clean build; the centre search found it unsound). `maxStages` caps the work per call (tests pass it with budgetMs = Infinity). */
  prepareCut?(budgetMs: number, maxStages?: number): boolean | 'thin' | 'loose';

### 17.2 Questions for the owner (genuine product choices; each with a recommendation; the build proceeds on the recommended default)

1. **When you join two pieces that were not cut apart from each other (piece 1 and piece 4 of six), what should happen?** A (recommended, and what is built): the piece they were both cut from comes back whole, with everything cut from it ("Joined 3 pieces into one."), so the result is always a real part of the squishy and always a valid body. **The cost, measured (revision 2, 50 species, random trees of five and six pieces, every pair):** over all pairs the join brings back exactly the two pieces for 14.6 %, three for 14.3 %, four for 13.9 %, five for 14.0 % and all six (the whole squishy) for 43.3 %. For pieces that really touch across a cut face (73 % of pairs), exactly the two join for 19.9 % (the two halves of one cut), three for 17.4 %, four for 13.9 %, five for 13.5 % and six for 35.4 %; the whole squishy comes back for 43 % of touching pairs, 54 % of touching pairs that are not the two halves of one cut. So joining two odd neighbours often puts a lot back. B: just those two stick into one lump (their summed volume); needs a union builder that, as built in the prototype, makes the wrong shape (IoU 0.84 median, 0.42 minimum, 39 % ragged rims) and is NOT shippable now; tuning item T9 builds it properly with a quality gate and falls back to A, for about 4 to 6 PHYS days; the first playtest decides whether it is worth it. C: refuse with a small wobble and "These two don't fit". The game says what will happen before it does ("Joining 3 pieces."), Undo cut steps back one cut at a time, and Reconnect all remains.
2. **If you stretch a piece's edge until it touches another piece and let go, should they join?** Today (and in CUT.md) yes, if the stretch actually touched it: "or let go while they touch". Because pieces now rest flush against each other, a stretch-and-release is more likely to end touching than it was with the old chunks. Default and recommendation: ship it as CUT.md says (`RELEASE_JOIN = 'touching'`, a gate that a flick never joins, and a stretch that never touched does not join: revision 2), and flip one constant to `'carried'` (only holding 0.4 s with the finger down, or lifting a piece and setting it down gently, joins) if the first playtest shows accidental joins.
3. **After a cut, should the camera swing a few degrees toward the new cut face?** Because the swipe plane contains the camera, a fresh cut face is seen exactly edge-on from the camera that made the cut; you see two clean silhouettes with an eye on each, and the flat faces appear as soon as a piece tilts or you orbit. Recommendation: yes, up to 8 degrees over about 0.35 s in and 1.2 s out, once per cut, never in Calm effects and never while you are orbiting (0.5 day; strike it without touching anything else). Revision 2 adds a reason: for the 43 % of swipes that are near-level (6.5), the peek is one of the two levers if the parting reads as nothing happening.
4. **Should Split in two cut along the squishy's own line between the eyes even when you have orbited the camera?** **Recommendation FLIPPED in revision 2: yes, for the FIRST Split in two on a whole squishy** (the squishy's own line between the eyes, whatever the camera shows), because the camera-relative version separated the eyes only within about 15 degrees of facing the squishy (45 of 50 species at 15 degrees, 2 of 50 at 20, 0 from 30 to 90) and "one eye on each side" is your example; later presses and swipes stay camera-relative ("what you see is what you cut"). One constant (`SPLIT_FIRST = 'body' | 'camera'`) switches it back.
5. **Undo cut: a button that joins the two halves of the newest cut.** New in revision 2 (red team PR-14). Recommendation: yes (built by default, 0.5 day): with single-cell pieces it is always an exact twin join, it gives a keyboard player a way back that is not "everything", and a touch player who cut by mistake does not have to find two pieces and drag. It adds a third button to the Cut popover.
6. **Your other note of 2026-10-08, "Shift does NOT lock the hold", is not built; when?** The agreed order is TRUE CUT first, then LOCK, which puts LOCK about 5 to 5.5 weeks away. Alternative: LOCK's input half (`src/input`, `driver.ts`) starts now against a mock (no conflict, recommended in both cases), and LOCK's physics half (`updateGrabs`) starts on the second PHYS engineer once the constructor refactor P2 has landed, at the cost of merge care in the one 2841-line `softbody.ts`. Recommendation: yes, start LOCK's physics half after P2, if a second PHYS engineer exists; otherwise keep the order.
